# AVSL handoff — 2026-10-19
**For the next ChatGPT/agent conversation.** Last completed test: 20-site frozen-amplitude, Marshall→post-Krylov transfer and few-shot adaptation. This is a **research checkpoint**, not an established scalable method.

## Project goal
Develop a sign-improvement procedure for frustrated spin-1/2 J1–J2 Heisenberg near J2/J1=0.5 that works beyond 20 spins / 4x4 and ultimately 6x6, 8x8, and larger. The algorithm should:
1. Discover *rare, energetically valuable* sign defects from bounded walker-accessible proposals, including Hamiltonian neighbors.
2. Query the local Krylov field and/or physically justified local energy evidence **only where worth its cost**; never pretend globally stored FN amplitudes are available at large N.
3. Verify candidate corrections robustly with imperfect amplitude ratios, avoid false positives and simultaneous-flip overcorrection, and retain reversibility.
4. Generalize each **iteration's updated composite sign function** with a neural policy/sign model; do not train directly on ED ground-state signs or stack difficult correction-factor networks.
5. Measure cost per genuine physical energy gain and local sign-edge quality; check **size consistency** against the 2026-10-13 local/region Krylov proposal. Merely improving CNN classification on 4x4/20 sites does not meet the goal.

## Conceptual recalibration and literature
The angle is named **Active Verifier-Guided Sign Learning (AVSL)**, inspired by on-policy DAgger, OpenAI process supervision and weak-to-strong generalization, Anthropic Constitutional AI / AI-feedback and reward-proxy failure, prioritized replay, GRPO (DeepSeekMath) and DAPO (ByteDance/Tsinghua). Read `README.md`, `LITERATURE.md`, `GRPO_DAPO_DYNAMIC_SAMPLING.md`. Main lessons:
- DAPO's filtering homogeneous binary-reward groups does *not* fix lack of proposal support; rejected trials cost evaluations. Explicit zero-reward **keep signs** anchors are essential; least-bad negative sign flips must not be rewarded as actual gains.
- A locally lower **fixed-amplitude physical variational energy** is not proof of better post-FN energy, true ED signs, or better network generalization.
- The alternative idea is *learn where to spend expensive Krylov evaluations*, not assume we must learn a globally accurate FN amplitude.
- Personal Context helped retrieve past failures and relevant earlier design; **it did not independently validate physics**.

## Background results (20-site and 4x4)
- 20-site sector has 184,756 computational configurations. FN suppression is enriched in wrong/flip signs, but not a valid flip label. CNN training with suppression sampling did not outperform ordinary training (5 seeds, 3k steps).
- An on-demand exact-FN-amplitude oracle allowed signed-bond-prioritized screening to get ~3.2x random-selection fixed-a gain at a comparable query budget (12 toy seeds). This is a *scoring* proof, not scalable learning.
- Synthetic independent log-amplitude errors ruined the naive energy gate: at noise sigma=0.3, 11/12 trials worsened actual energy. Two successive exact fixed-sign FN amplitudes retained energy benefit in 12/12; correlated errors matter.
- Walker-only NCE amplitude learning from FN mixed samples improved amplitude ratios but not enough on correction bonds: with 128 walkers, RMS log ratio ~0.557 bulk, ~0.638 correction-edge; only 1/4 local sign trials improved energy, mean fixed-a gain negative.
- 4x4 first learned correction-priority policy looked better than random and signed-bond heuristic but geometry-only feature ablation removed *no* performance: likely small-lattice shortcut; label acquisition cost > full 4x4 orbit coverage.
- Earlier 6x6 raw K2 sometimes harmed energy. Local/region Krylov vs single global threshold may be essential for size consistency; see `projects/j1j2/DESIGN_scalable_loop_2026-10-13.md`.

## Latest 20-site chamber-transfer test — *complete*
A learned candidate selector was trained on **Marshall** signs and tested without retraining on **post-Krylov** signs, while **sharing one frozen exact FN amplitude vector**, same walker-seeded frontiers, symmetry-orbit holdout and selection budget. Training: 18 episodes x 110 = **1,980 labeled orbit queries** (plus amplitude calls). Evaluation: 20 held-out seeds, **22 ranked orbits** per episode, sequential greedily accepted fixed-a orbit corrections.
Mean actual frozen-guide energy decrease:
| Selector | Marshall | Post-Krylov |
|---|---:|---:|
| Learned bond-feature policy (trained Marshall) | +0.00024914 | +0.000001036 |
| Geometry/amplitude-only network | +0.00002862 | +0.000001774 |
| Observed signed-bond heuristic | +0.00072988 | 0 |
| Random | +0.00017051 | +0.000000320 |
Single-orbit profitable opportunities fall **1,458 → 201** from Marshall to post-Krylov (same fixed a). An oracle selecting the best held-out orbits could extract mean **~0.0000325** post-Krylov gain per evaluation episode, so *some* benefit remains missed by zero-shot transfer. Note: these are **fixed-a** energies, not FN-projected ground-state energy changes.

## Mixed and few-shot transfer — *complete*
- A single policy trained on 990 Marshall + 990 post-Krylov labels (same 1,980 total labeled training orbits) improves post-Krylov gain vs Marshall-only to **~0.0000127** (20 held-out episodes). But a policy trained directly on all 1,980 post-Krylov examples reaches **~0.0000217–0.0000225**; geometry-only direct training can do even better (**~0.0000286**). Mixed-chamber training only partially transfers.
- Fine-tuning a Marshall-pretrained model on post-Krylov labels (two NN initializations): **108 labels (4 positives)** -> mean gain **0.0000108**; **360 labels (8 positives)** -> **0.0000149**; **720 labels (12 positives)** -> **0.0000153**; **1,980 labels (49 positives)** -> **0.0000225**. Scratch-trained on 360 target labels -> **~0.0000124**. The ~20% warm-vs-scratch gain is a tiny 20-site, 2-seed result: **do not claim robust amortization**; source-pretraining cost (1,980 labels), all frontier generation and exact-oracle queries were **NOT** included in the comparison.
- Energies were directly checked against full Hamiltonian for 64 few-shot flips (max discrepancy ~2.6e-14), and 160 checked mixed-chamber flips (~3e-14). This validates fixed-a crossing-bond energy accounting, not physical scalability.
- Feature ablation warns the learned bond features can be redundant with local geometry/amplitude. Need evaluate genuinely new sign chambers and realistic imperfect, callable guides.

## Next experiment / decision
**Do not just scale a frozen Marshall policy to 6x6.** Before any large run, test real *on-policy adaptation* in a fresh post-Krylov/FN sign chamber using **only walker-accessible and callable sign/amplitude information**, with source pretraining + exploration + verification + fine-tuning all charged to one total budget. Critically compare: (A) fixed signed-bond prioritizer, (B) geometry-only model, (C) transferred policy and few-shot update, (D) new-chamber trained-from-scratch, and (E) keep guide. Use a separate holdout, independent physical-H/FN evaluator and local edge sign quality. Train **the explorer first**, then composite sign network if verified exploration actually beats the baselines. Consider an explicit stop/fail gate if costly source pretraining never amortizes.
