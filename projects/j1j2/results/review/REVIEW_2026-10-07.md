# Referee-style review of the FN/Krylov loop programme (2026-10-07)

Independent, read-only review of `RESEARCH_MAP.md`, `CLAUDE_TAKE.md` and the evidence folders listed there. I re-derived the
load-bearing numbers from the JSONs and the code. Energies are per site. Severity: **critical** = undermines a headline claim or
the viability case; **major** = changes an inference or a stated number; **minor** = presentation or bookkeeping.

## A. Correctness of the load-bearing claims

**1. [critical] At a VMC-stationary network the loop's amplitude step adds nothing at first order. The "projection, not
information" verdict is close to a tautology.**
- For any sign-s state b, lattice FN gives E_f[b] := <b|H_FN[a,s]|b>/<b|b> >= <H>_b, with equality at b = a.
  So g = E_f - <H> is minimal at a, and grad E_f(a) = grad <H>(a).
- Hence at a network that is stationary for fixed-sign VMC, the frozen-FN objective is stationary too, inside the same family.
  The design memo's "FN surrogate = one majorise-minimise step" and writeback `DESIGN.md` 1.5.3 already contain this.
- Consequences:
  - The exact loop's "plentiful information" (`stall_6x6`: 4.8e-5 in the first iteration) is just unrestricted (15.8M-dof)
    energy minimisation.
  - Inside a network, the loop can beat continued VMC of the *same capacity* only through sign changes, added parameters,
    or better conditioning.
  - AMP6 is exactly this: the loop arm ties control D, -0.35(0.31)e-5. T3 is a 1.9-sigma tie.
- Every amplitude experiment therefore needs a same-capacity, same-sample plain-VMC control. The write-back pre-registration
  (`experiments/writeback/README.md`) does not have one (finding 9).

**2. [critical] The 4x4 headline "loop beats the best tuned VMC 3.3-7.2x at equal CPU-h" is not a fair or transferable result.**
I checked `vit/*.json` and `vit_rgn_4x4.py`. The ratios themselves are arithmetically right (6.11e-5/2.04e-4 ... 9.0e-6/6.5e-5).
- *Sign oracle.*
  - The loop's sign is an energy-optimal threshold over the full basis: a 12870-entry table, about 1 s, w_s -> 0.
  - The control must carry its phase in the 23,680 network parameters.
  - The net-sign arm (32-47%) shows the sign table is where the gain comes from.
  - At 6x6 the same step costs about 1 GPU-h per iteration and is lossy.
- *CPU-h is an implementation artefact.* Both arms build full-basis Jacobians, and the complex arm also does full-basis
  complex matvecs inside every CG product (`Mmv`, complex mode).
  - Measured step times: complex 118-190 s, rising to 640-1070 s after N doubles; loop steps 20-35 s.
  - At **equal step count** the advantage is 1.2-1.8x. After 5 steps: loop 6.2e-5 / 6.8e-5 vs complex 7.2e-5 / 1.24e-4.
  - The late-time factor comes from the control stalling at its noise floor, about 5.8e-5.
  - The non-adaptive loop stalls at the same level (5.0-6.6e-5).
- *Seeds.* 2 loop seeds, and 1 seed for the adaptive complex and net-sign arms.
- Remove this claim from "Established". At most claim: "with exact signs, fixed-sign second-order VMC converges faster than
  complex VMC on 4x4" (expected).

**3. [critical] The one-hop budget is better spent on a Lanczos step; nobody has compared the two.**
- The composite-net + hop sign needs psi on all one-hop neighbours. So does psi_1 = (1 + alpha H) psi.
  Both need the two-hop shell for a local energy. Same cost class.
- Literature, 6x6 (`literature/J1J2_BENCHMARKS_COST.md`): symmetrised RBM -0.50364 -> one Lanczos step -0.50376
  (variational, +1.2e-4).
- Ours:
  - The Krylov sign step at psi_P gains 5e-6 (`sym_test0.json`).
  - The best learned 6x6 guide, -0.503662(22) (T3), is 1.0e-4 above RBM + 1 Lanczos step.
- The sign-only step discards the amplitude half of the same hop. Until it beats Lanczos at equal hop cost, the
  "enhance your NQS" pitch is not supported.

**4. [major] "0.004 rms" is a white-noise artefact, not a requirement.**
- 0.33 sigma^2 = (2/3)|E/N| sigma^2 is the exchange (kinetic) energy per site. It is the cost of *iid per-orbit* noise
  (`interp_fn_update.json`; derived in writeback `DESIGN.md` 1.2).
- The real criterion is the edge Dirichlet form Q(e) <~ 5e-6 (`DESIGN.md` 1.3). By that measure the update delta itself
  is 0.30x white noise.
- `RESEARCH_MAP` §3.1, `CLAUDE_TAKE` and the `stall_6x6` README still state 0.004 rms as the target. Correct them.
- The "intensive, same at 10x10" argument is right only for edge differences. A network's per-configuration log error is
  extensive, and its rms grows with N at fixed local accuracy. Stating the tolerance on edge differences is the correct
  (and more favourable) form.

**5. [major] The evidence that "the network cannot absorb the update" is weaker than stated.**
- *L2-projected loop* (`proj_loop_l2.json`):
  - It starts from the rep-evaluated ViT, which is itself rough: 2.09e-4 vs 1.32e-4 for psi_P, i.e. 7.7e-5 of roughness,
    more than one iteration's gain.
  - The fit did not converge: validation loss 3.36e-4 -> 3.21e-4 (about 2% in rms), guide <H> 2.09e-4 -> 2.06e-4.
  - "Projection error 0.012 = gain" therefore means the fit returned the start.
- *Exact-verified SR* (`t1_fit_exact_vit.json`):
  - Also on the rep-ViT, with N_sr = 6144 << P = 155k.
  - The first SR try predicted -0.11 and measured +5.5e-4. That is an unreliable quadratic model, not proof of a
    "stiff landscape".
- Only the small families (44 clusters, 7 hop features) were tested properly on psi_P.
- The `stall_6x6` README hedge ("not settled") is right. The map's "the network cannot absorb" is too strong.

**6. [major] The FN/DMC referee has a bias at the size of the claimed effects.**
- `stall_6x6` now gives exact E_FN for the three oracle guides. DMC (M = 128) minus exact: -8.3e-6, -2.0e-5, -1.5e-5.
  All three are negative; the weighted mean is -1.1(0.7)e-5, consistent with negative population-control bias.
- Every 6x6 claim was of that size: T3 -8.2(4.3)e-6; it2 -1.7(2.5)e-5; K3vit -7.1(3.0)e-5 at M = 128, gone at M = 512.
- Medians (the RESEARCH_MAP §4 rule) are not unbiased estimators of E_FN for skewed population distributions.
- The 8x8 verdict ("M = 128 is sufficient", 2 replicas) is uncontrolled.

**7. [major] Symmetrisation is neither free nor resolved.**
- psi_P (exact, -0.5036777) vs the ViT VMC energy (-0.503654(21)) is a 2.4(2.1)e-5 difference: 1.1 sigma.
- Projection multiplies every evaluation by |G| = 16. That includes every hop.
- The "1.7e-4 for the public 10x10 ViT" compares *separately trained* checkpoints (`main` vs `symm_trxy_ising`).
  It is not a post-hoc projection, and the latter costs 3317 vs 41 s per sweep, about 80x.
- Symmetry projection is standard (Nomura; Rende; Roth; Chen-Heyl). Use it, but do not count it as a loop result.

**8. [major] The sign-learnability and "6x better signs" claims are over-read.**
- All scaling data use the exact amplitude, exact labels and an exact global T. Only N = 36 is in the generalisation regime
  (9.5% orbit coverage); 32 -> 36 is a single step.
- w_s is not an energy measure: at the psi_P amplitude, the exact signs give a *higher* <H> (1.56e-4 vs 1.32e-4).
- "Signs 6x better than the ViT" (2e-5 vs 1.3e-4) bought nothing energetically, and must not be a headline.

**9. [major] The write-back plan (`results/writeback/`) is a representability test with oracle data, and it lacks the decisive control.**
- `wb_run.py` draws x ~ phi_FN^2 exactly and uses exact phi_FN at x and at every neighbour inside the loss (`dl`, `w`).
  A real loop has neither.
- The missing arm, **V-M**: the same residual CNN trained on <H> at fixed sign (and at the Krylov sign), with the same
  steps and samples. If V-M matches E-M, the FN target adds nothing (finding 1).
- Even PASS (<H> <= 1.08e-4) leaves the guide 6e-5 above RBM+PP.
- My prior: PARTIAL. The 7 hop features got 8%, and delta is 0.3x white-noise rough.

**10. [minor]**
- (a) "3 exact iterations from psi_P reach RBM+PP" compares an unrestricted 15.8M-entry table with a compact ansatz. It is
  an information bound, not a method result.
- (b) The T3 numbers in `DESIGN_MEMO.md` (-0.6(0.3)e-5, -0.503660(21)) disagree with `T3_table.md`
  (-8.2(4.3)e-6, -0.503662(22)).
- (c) "Iterations flat 16 -> 36" holds in exact arithmetic only. The sampled 4x4 loop had a 1/N_samples floor
  (2.2e-4 at 1e8 samples per iteration on a 12870-state space).
- (d) Bad-start results (eigenstates are fixed points; sectors trap) follow from FN theory. The new part is the escape law
  ln(1/w)/0.35, measured on the exact loop only.
- (e) The 8x8 verdict calls K1 "scaling positively", although E_FN(K1) = -0.49821 is 6e-4 above the ViT.

## B. Scaling and cost

Assumptions, scaled from measured 6x6 numbers:
- One-hop count n1 ~ 2.4N (85 at N = 36); two-hop shell n2 ~ 2.6N^2 (3315 at N = 36).
- Network cost per evaluation is proportional to N at fixed width, x2.5 for the 434k-parameter 10x10 ViT.
- AMP6: 1e5 zero-hop samples per 20 A40-min. Amplitude steps need N_s >= P (AMP6 generalisation), 3 steps per iteration.
- A40-h ~ A100-h within 2x.
- FN referee at 1e-5/site with a hop guide: about 20 A40-h at 6x6, extrapolated from 32 populations at M = 512
  (2.6 A40-h, +-2.9e-5). Cost scales as N^3 with a hop guide and N^2 with a zero-hop guide.

| per iteration | 6x6 | 8x8 | 10x10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 amplitude steps (zero-hop, N_s = 1.5e5 / 3e5 / 4e5) | ~1.5 | ~9 | ~75 |
| sign step + distillation (hop labels, T) | ~1 | ~5 | ~45 |
| **iteration total (A40-h)** | **~2.5 (measured 4-5 GPU-h, 2080 Ti)** | **~15** | **~120** |
| FN referee, 1e-5/site, hop guide (once) | ~20 | ~110 | ~450-1100 |
| FN referee, zero-hop guide (once) | ~0.5 | ~2 | ~10-20 |

Totals:
- **From a strong symmetrised NQS** (3 iterations, if write-back worked): 8x8 ~50 A40-h, 10x10 ~400 A40-h, plus the referee.
  - That is cheap next to 1.9e3-3.8e3 A100-h, but the guide's training cost is sunk and not counted.
  - The gain is speculative. The exact loop's first iteration removes about 30% of the error, about 1.4e-5 at 10x10 from
    the HF ViT. That is at the referee's resolution, while the record's error bar is 4e-7.
- **From a bad start**: about 33 plain iterations to the RBM+PP level (6x6 exact history: eps < 9e-5 at iteration 33;
  22 to the ViT level). Anderson needs 12-15 but is unsafe from bad starts. That gives 8x8 ~500 and 10x10 ~4000 A40-h:
  the ViT's budget, with zero margin for imperfect write-back.
- Sample count: 33 x 3 x 4e5 = 4e7 vs about 8e7 for the 10x10 ViT, at the same per-sample cost. Because the amplitude step
  *is* a VMC step, the loop cannot be orders of magnitude cheaper. Its only lever is fewer, better-conditioned steps, and
  4x4 shows at most 1.8x per step.

Where it breaks:
- The write-back has zero first-order gain at stationarity (finding 1).
- Hop cost per sample grows as N^3 vs N^2 for VMC (finding 3).
- Sign distillation enters a zero-coverage regime at 8x8.
- The DMC referee bias is about 1e-5.

Possible niche:
- (i) Sign repair for guides with *bad* signs and good amplitudes, e.g. J2 > 0.5 or non-Marshall lattices. The 4x4
  net-sign arm shows the lever is large only when w_s is large.
- (ii) A calibrated FN upper bound on a strong zero-hop guide at 8x8 or 12x12. This is cheap, and new if it comes in
  below -0.49889 (8x8).

## C. Novelty and framing

**New and publishable now:**
- Exact symmetric-sector FN, Krylov and variational energies for arbitrary NQS guides at 36 sites on one GPU, as a
  diagnostic and calibration tool.
- The composite-vs-factor sign-learnability result, with one-hop repair, as a methods note (context: Westerhout et al. 2020;
  Szabo & Castelnovo 2020; Bukov et al. 2021).
- A clean negative result: on a strong NQS the FN/Krylov loop is limited by VMC capacity (finding 1).

**Known:**
- The sign of (T - H)psi is the sign of a Lanczos/power step (Sorella PRB 64, 024512 (2001); Hu et al. 2013).
- Iterating nodes from FN projections is self-healing DMC (Reboredo, Hood & Kent, PRB 79, 195117 (2009); I did not
  re-verify this reference here).
- Optimising a guide from FN walkers: FN-SR (Sorella & Capriotti 2000).
- The "majorisation identity" is the ten Haaf et al. (1995) variational theorem.
- Supervised power-method NQS: Kochkov & Clark; Jonsson et al.; Giuliani et al.; Ledinauskas; Sinibaldi; Gravina.
- Symmetry projection: see finding 7.

**Do not claim:**
- "Beats VMC 3-7x".
- Any "beats/matches the ViT" result.
- "Symmetrisation is free".
- "Iterations do not grow with N" for a sampled loop.
- w_s improvements as energy improvements.
- "First FN with a deep-NQS guide" before a calibrated 8x8+ number.

## D. Recommendations
**Do next:**
1. **Add arm V-M to the write-back test** (same residual net trained on <H> at the s_P and Krylov signs; same steps and
   samples; exact sector). Pre-register: if V-M captures >= 80% of E-M's gain, drop the FN amplitude target from the pitch.
2. **Exact 6x6 Lanczos comparison at equal hop cost**:
   - <H> and E_FN of psi_P + alpha H psi_P (alpha optimised);
   - vs (|psi_P|, Krylov sign) and composite + hop.
   This decides whether the add-on is "sign-only Krylov" or "Lanczos + FN".
3. **Calibrate the DMC referee against the exact 6x6 FN**: M = 128 to 2048, beta, mean vs median vs trimmed mean, on the
   three oracle guides plus psi_P. Fix one protocol. Then report cost per sample and A100-h for every arm, sign and hop
   costs included.

**Stop:** running amplitude-update variants on the 6x6 ViT that have no same-capacity plain-VMC control and rest on
M = 128 DMC differences of about 1e-5.

## Verdict on viability
As a ground-state solver at 8x8-10x10 the loop is not viable on current evidence:
- its amplitude step is a VMC step;
- write-back has zero first-order gain at a converged network;
- hop-signed evaluation and referee costs grow as N^3;
- from a bad start it costs as much as training a ViT, even with perfect write-back.

A narrow add-on role (sign repair for sign-poor guides; a calibrated FN bound on a strong guide) survives only if it beats a
plain Lanczos step at equal hop cost.

## E. Addendum 2026-10-09: one-hop-input write-back, the 6x6 loop, and the k-hop recursion

Sources: RESEARCH_MAP §3h-3j; `writeback_tail/README.md` §6-6d; `SCALING_PLAN.md`; `itfit_6x6`; `lanczos_baseline_6x6`.

**E1. [critical] The 6x6 loop still loses to the baseline this project has already measured.**
- One exact Lanczos step on psi_P gives <H> 2.55e-5 and E_FN 1.89e-5. It is a callable one-hop object and needs no
  training.
- wtLOOP8 after 8 iterations: 3.16e-5 / 2.75e-5. That guide is a 15.8M-entry table; made callable it would be an
  8-hop object.
- The only callable variant measured (6d, option B) plateaus at 5.6-6.2e-5, above RBM+PP.
- So "passes RBM+PP" is a table result. Every loop number must be quoted against Lanczos p1/p2 at the same hop count.

**E2. [major] Further holes in the 6x6 loop claims.**
- (a) *Table storage.* f is evaluated on all orbits with exact table features; only the training data are realistic.
- (b) *Exact global Krylov sign every iteration.* This is oracle-cheap, as on 4x4. Also report the trajectory with the
  amplitude steps only.
- (c) *One trajectory per setting, GPU runs not reproducible.* The per-iteration capture differs by up to 27 points
  between wtLOOP and wtLOOP8 (iteration 5: 68.5% vs 41.8%). Report >= 3 seeds with their spread.
- (d) *The VMC controls are matched in samples, not in guide evaluations.* The FN target spends a hop per sample.
  Adam at B = 256, with energy-estimate noise 200x the signal, is a weak control. The claimed edge is the
  zero-variance per-configuration target, and that edge is not FN-specific. The proper control is the same FEAT net
  regressed onto the one-hop Lanczos/ITE target log(1 + alpha(E_L - E)). If that ties, the loop is a supervised
  Lanczos step (Kochkov-Clark; Giuliani et al.).

**E3. Is there a conceptual reason distillation must fail? No.**
- The stack is a function of x alone. V and W need a at the neighbours only because the net is evaluated pointwise.
- The real obstacle is precision. A zero-hop net has to reproduce the base's finite differences (log-ratios on about
  135 bonds) to about 3e-3 down to phi^2 ~ 1e-14.
- Fitting values does not control differences (spectral bias). That is why spin-only re-basing kept 10.6% / 2.6%
  even with the edge loss (6d(a)).
- So test the log-ratios implied by any candidate architecture, not only the energy it keeps.

**E4. Architecture.**
- *Avoid:*
  - message passing on the spin-swap graph: that is hopping, about 135 base calls per layer;
  - a warm-started ViT: fine-tuning the converged ViT is the documented failure (ViT minSR arm: +6% on the validation
    estimate, -22% exact).
- *Prefer a bond-output ratio net* R(x) = {log a(x^b) - log a(x)} over all bonds b, in one forward pass: a lattice
  CNN/GNN with a per-bond head.
  - Train it supervised on exact base pairs. Labels cost 2 evaluations, data are unlimited, and tail orbits can be
    held out.
  - Then f_k = h_k(x, log a_k, V_k, W_k from R_k), and R_{k+1} = R_k + [f_k(x^b) - f_k(x)] is distilled each
    iteration from one hop of small nets.
  - Guide cost grows linearly in k, with no 135^k recursion. The base is called once per configuration.
- *Cheaper first try:* input-gradient features. One backward pass gives a linearised log-ratio for every bond.

**E5. Pre-registered pre-tests, exact 6x6, in this order.**
- (i) *Sensitivity.* Replay wtLOOP8 iterations 1-3 with V, W multiplied by exp(eta xi), eta = 0.01 / 0.03 / 0.1.
  The capture-vs-eta curve fixes the required accuracy eta*.
- (ii) *Ratio fidelity.* rms log-ratio error of R on held-out orbits, per phi^2 decade. Pass if it is <= eta* in every
  decade down to 1e-14.
- (iii) *End-to-end, no table anywhere.* 3 and 6 iterations with a callable guide (base + chain of small nets), 3 seeds.
  Pass needs all of:
  - >= 70% of the table loop's accumulated gain;
  - evaluation cost <= 3 base forwards per configuration;
  - callable guide + Lanczos p1 below psi_P + Lanczos p1 (2.55e-5) by >= 2x.
- *On your planned distillation test (>= 70% of the accumulated <H> gain):* necessary but not sufficient. Also measure
  the distilled guide's log-ratio fidelity, and continue the loop 2 iterations from it. Its V and W drive the next
  target, and 70% of the energy can coexist with poor ratios.

**E6. Avoiding the recursion altogether.**
- Cap the depth: frozen-base B for 2 iterations, then a final exact Lanczos step and calibrated FN.
- At 8x8, compare that first against ViT + Lanczos p1/p2 + FN. That baseline needs no training.
- If the loop cannot beat it at equal hops, the recursion question does not matter.

**Updated verdict.** Write-back has moved from "fails" to "works with one-hop inputs on tables" (a real advance). It is
not yet shown for a callable guide or against Lanczos. My viability verdict changes only if E5(iii) passes.
