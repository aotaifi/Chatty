\title{Reconstructing frustrated signs from amplitudes with fixed-node and Krylov updates}

\author{A. Otaifi}
\affiliation{Arnold Sommerfeld Center for Theoretical Physics, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit\"at M\"unchen, 80333 M\"unchen, Germany}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We split a real wave function into signs and positive amplitudes,
$\psi=s\,a$, and update them alternately in the frustrated square-lattice
$J_1$--$J_2$ model at $J_2/J_1=0.5$. The signs follow from a label-free
Krylov step, $s'=s\,\operatorname{sgn}[T-(H\psi)/\psi]$; the amplitude from
lattice fixed node, which cannot raise the energy. Given the exact amplitude,
three to four Krylov steps recover the exact ground-state signs on a
$4\times4$ cluster for $0.4\leq J_2/J_1\leq1$. Alternating both updates with
exact fixed node converges to the exact ground state on $4\times4$ and
20-site clusters, about three times faster with Anderson acceleration. On
$6\times6$ and $8\times8$, Krylov signs built from a neural-network amplitude
reduce the fixed-node energy gap to the network from $4\times10^{-3}$ to
$6\times10^{-4}$ per site, without closing it. Iterating further requires
learning the fixed-node amplitude from walkers, which remains the open problem.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}

The Monte Carlo sign problem is severe in frustrated spin systems
\cite{HeneliusSandvik2000,TroyerWiese2005}. We use the square-lattice
$J_1$--$J_2$ Heisenberg model as a benchmark. At $J_2=0$ its ground-state
signs obey the Marshall rule \cite{Marshall1955}; near $J_2/J_1=1/2$ that
rule fails and accurate variational states become nontrivial
\cite{Choo2019,ChenHeyl2024}.

We ask whether signs and positive amplitudes can be improved separately.
For a real wave function we write
\begin{equation}
\psi(x)=s(x)a(x),\qquad s(x)=\pm1,\quad a(x)=|\psi(x)|>0 .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
Given $a$, a single Krylov step updates the signs. Given $s$, lattice fixed
node gives a sign-free positive-amplitude problem \cite{vanBemmel1994}.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines both updates. Section~\ref{sec:results}
tests them in order of increasing difficulty: first the sign update alone
with an exact amplitude, then the exact feedback loop, then larger lattices,
and finally the open problem of learning the amplitude from fixed-node
walkers.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Model and error measures}

We study
\begin{equation}
H=J_1\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j
 +J_2\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle}
 \mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j ,
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
on periodic lattices with $J_1=1$ in the $S^z=0$ sector. For a spin
configuration $x$, the Marshall rule is $s_M(x)=(-1)^{N^A_\downarrow(x)}$ up
to an overall sign, where $N^A_\downarrow$ counts down spins on one
checkerboard sublattice.

When the exact ground state $\psi_0=s_0a_0$ is available, we fix the
irrelevant overall sign and measure the sign error by the wrong-sign
probability
\begin{equation}
w_s=\sum_{x:\,s(x)\neq s_0(x)} a_0(x)^2 ,
\label{eq:signweight}
\end{equation}
and the energy error by $\epsilon_{\rm rel}=(E-E_0)/|E_0|$.

\subsection{One-step Krylov sign update}

For $\psi=sa$, define the local energy
\begin{equation}
r_s(x)=\frac{(H\psi)(x)}{\psi(x)} .
\label{eq:localenergy}
\end{equation}
Because $[(T-H)\psi](x)=[T-r_s(x)]\psi(x)$, the sign of the shifted Krylov
vector is
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm K}(x)=s(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r_s(x)] :
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
the sign of every configuration with $r_s(x)>T$ is flipped. Only this sign
is kept; the amplitude factor $|T-r_s(x)|$ is discarded, so the method is not
repeated Lanczos multiplication. The threshold $T$ never uses the target
signs. For exact systems, $T$ minimizes the variational energy of
$a(x)s_{\rm K}(x)$ over all thresholds. For sampled systems, $T$ is obtained
from a two-cluster split of the sampled $r_s(x)$ values.

\subsection{Fixed-node amplitude update}

For a trial wave function $\psi_T$, an off-diagonal matrix element connecting
$x$ and $y$ is sign violating when $H_{xy}\psi_T(y)/\psi_T(x)>0$. Lattice
fixed node removes such terms and adds their contribution to the diagonal
\cite{vanBemmel1994}. The resulting Hamiltonian is sign free, keeps the
signs of $\psi_T$ fixed, and its ground-state energy $E_\FN$ is a variational
upper bound. Let $\phi_\FN(x)>0$ be its positive ground-state amplitude. In an
exact small-system calculation, $\phi_\FN$ replaces $a$ directly before the
next application of Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}.

\subsection{Accelerating the amplitude iteration}

With exact fixed node, the loop is a fixed-point iteration for the amplitude,
$x_{k+1}=g(x_k)$ with $x=\log a$ and $g(x_k)=\log\phi_\FN[a_k,s_k]$. We
accelerate it with Anderson mixing \cite{Anderson1965,WalkerNi2011}: with the
residuals $f_k=g(x_k)-x_k$ and the differences $\Delta f_j$, $\Delta g_j$ of the
last $m$ iterates,
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}=g(x_k)-\sum_{j}\gamma_j\,\Delta g_j,\qquad
\gamma=\arg\min_\gamma\Big\|f_k-\sum_j\gamma_j\Delta f_j\Big\| .
\label{eq:anderson}
\end{equation}
We use $m=3$ and clear the history whenever the fixed-node energy rises or a
step becomes too large. The
sign step is unchanged.

\subsection{Learning the amplitude from walkers}

In a walker calculation $\phi_\FN$ is not available as a function. The
stationary walker distribution is instead the mixed distribution
\begin{equation}
f(x)\propto a(x)\phi_\FN(x) .
\label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
If a positive model is trained by maximum likelihood with
$p_\theta(x)\propto a_\theta(x)^2$, the exact maximum-likelihood target is
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm new}^2(x)\propto a(x)\phi_\FN(x),
\label{eq:halfstep}
\end{equation}
the geometric mean of the current and fixed-node amplitudes. We
precondition the likelihood gradient $g$ with stochastic reconfiguration
(SR),
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta=(S+\lambda I)^{-1}g ,
\label{eq:sr}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the quantum geometric tensor of $\log a_\theta$.

\subsection{Numerical setup}

Exact calculations use two periodic clusters (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattices}): the
$4\times4$ square ($N=16$, $12\,870$ states with $S^z=0$) and a tilted 20-site
torus with periodic vectors $(4,0)$ and $(1,5)$.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig1_lattices.pdf}
\caption{Exact clusters with periodic boundaries: (a) $4\times4$, (b) tilted
20-site torus. Colours: Marshall sublattices; arrows: periodic vectors;
$J_1$/$J_2$ bonds shown for one site.}
\label{fig:lattices}
\end{figure}
 For $6\times6$ and $8\times8$
we use a previously optimized translationally invariant vision-transformer
neural quantum state (ViT) at $J_2/J_1=0.5$. Fixed-node energies are mixed
estimates from walker populations of size $M$ and projection length
$\beta=1.2$; $6\times6$ uses $M=32$ and $M=128$, $8\times8$ uses $M=128$.
Statistical conventions are summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:stats}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Krylov sign update}

We test the sign update alone, holding the amplitude fixed, with amplitudes
of decreasing quality: exact, approximate, and a neural network.

\subsubsection{Exact amplitude}

The first benchmark uses the exact $4\times4$ cluster:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Guide.} Take the exact ground-state amplitude
$a_0(x)=|\psi_0(x)|$ from exact diagonalization and give it the Marshall
sign, $\psi(x)=s_M(x)\,a_0(x)$. The amplitude is exact, so all error is in
the signs. (For $J_2/J_1\geq0.8$ the stripe sign replaces Marshall.)
\item \textbf{One Krylov sign step.} Compute $r(x)=(H\psi)(x)/\psi(x)$,
choose the threshold $T$ that minimizes the energy of $a_0 s'$, and set
$s'(x)=s_M(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r(x)]$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}. The amplitude
$a_0$ is not changed.
\item \textbf{Compare with exact diagonalization.} Measure the wrong-sign
probability $w_s$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:signweight}, and the relative energy error
$\epsilon_{\rm rel}$ of $\psi=s_M a_0$ (before) and $\psi'=s' a_0$ (after).
\item \textbf{Repeat.} Apply the Krylov sign step again to $s'a_0$, with a
new threshold computed from the new local energies, still keeping $a_0$
fixed and never using the exact signs.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig2_krylov_exact_amp.pdf}
\caption{Sign correction by Krylov steps, starting from Marshall signs. Only
the signs change; the amplitude stays the exact ED one. (a) $r(x)$ at
$J_2/J_1=0.5$; dashed line: threshold $T$. (b) Wrong-sign probability and
(c) energy error vs number of Krylov sign steps, compared with ED. (d) First
step on the $4\times4$ and 20-site clusters (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattices}).}
\label{fig:proof}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:proof}(a) shows why one step works at $J_2/J_1=0.5$. The
configurations on which the Marshall sign is wrong have systematically larger
local energies $r(x)$ than the correctly signed ones, and the threshold $T$
separates the two groups: $98\%$ of the wrong-sign probability lies at $r>T$.
One step lowers the wrong-sign probability from $1.27\times10^{-2}$ to
$3.41\times10^{-4}$, a factor $37$, and the relative energy error from
$2.19\times10^{-2}$ to $1.04\times10^{-3}$, a factor $21$. The same first
step gives factors $50$ and $24$ on the independent 20-site cluster
[Fig.~\ref{fig:proof}(d)].

One step is close to the ground state but not exact: the fidelity with
$\psi_0$ is $0.9986$. Repeating the step removes the remaining errors
[Fig.~\ref{fig:proof}(b,c)]. At $J_2/J_1=0.5$ the second step gives
$w_s=8.9\times10^{-7}$ and $\epsilon_{\rm rel}=2.5\times10^{-6}$, and the
third step reaches the exact ground-state signs. For every coupling from
$J_2/J_1=0.4$ to $1.0$, including the most frustrated point $0.6$ where the
Marshall guide has $10\%$ wrong-sign probability, at most four steps reach
the exact signs. With an exact amplitude, the sign problem on this cluster is
therefore solved by a few label-free Krylov steps. The question is what
happens when the amplitude is not exact.

\subsubsection{Approximate amplitude}

Next the amplitude is only approximate. Keeping the target $J_2/J_1=0.5$, we
use as fixed amplitude the exact ground-state amplitude of a different
coupling, $a(x)=|\psi_0^{J_2'}(x)|$ with $J_2'=0$--$0.6$, which gives a
physical, structured amplitude error. The guide again has Marshall signs, and
the Krylov steps are repeated as before.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig3_krylov_approx_amp.pdf}
\caption{Krylov sign steps on an approximate amplitude at $J_2/J_1=0.5$; the
amplitude is the exact one of $J_2'$ (fidelity $F$). (a) Sign and (b) energy
error vs number of Krylov sign steps, compared with ED.}
\label{fig:approx}
\end{figure}

With an approximate amplitude the steps still lower the energy, but they
converge to a nonzero error set by the amplitude [Fig.~\ref{fig:approx}(a,b)].
For an amplitude with fidelity $0.98$ ($J_2'=0.45$) the energy error drops
from $1.3\times10^{-2}$ to $3.4\times10^{-3}$, and the wrong-sign probability
from $1.3\times10^{-2}$ to $9\times10^{-4}$. Only the exact amplitude
reaches the exact signs; the residual error is set by the amplitude, which
motivates updating the amplitude itself (Sec.~\ref{sec:loop}).

\subsubsection{Neural-network amplitude on $6\times6$}
\label{sec:vit6}

On $6\times6$ no exact amplitude exists. We take the positive amplitude of
the optimized ViT state, give it Marshall signs, and apply one Krylov step.
The ViT's own signs serve as a reference.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig4_krylov_vit_6x6.pdf}
\caption{One Krylov sign step on the ViT amplitude ($6\times6$). No ED is
available, so the ViT signs are the reference. (a) Sign error and (b) energy
error per site of the Marshall guide and after one step.}
\label{fig:vit6}
\end{figure}

One Krylov step reduces the disagreement with the ViT signs by a factor
$20$--$23$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:vit6}(a)]. With the same amplitude, Marshall signs
lie $(5.3\pm1.6)\times10^{-3}$ per site above the ViT signs, while the Krylov
signs are statistically indistinguishable from them,
$e_{\rm Krylov}-e_{\rm ViT}=(1.2\pm2.9)\times10^{-4}$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:vit6}(b)]. A single label-free step thus recovers the
energetically relevant sign structure of an optimized neural network.

\subsection{Fixed-node--Krylov loop}
\label{sec:loop}

Krylov steps give the best signs for a given amplitude; fixed node gives an
improved amplitude for given signs, with $E_0\leq E_\FN\leq\langle H\rangle_T$
\cite{vanBemmel1994}. We alternate the two, here with exact diagonalization of
the fixed-node Hamiltonian (no sampling):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Guide.} Exact $J_2=0$ amplitude with Marshall signs, target
$J_2/J_1=0.5$. Krylov steps alone do not change these signs.
\item \textbf{Fixed node.} Diagonalize $H_\FN$ of the current guide; its
ground state $\phi_\FN$ is the new amplitude.
\item \textbf{Krylov sign step} on $\phi_\FN$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}.
\item \textbf{Repeat} from step 2.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig5_fn_krylov_loop.pdf}
\caption{Fixed-node--Krylov loop with exact diagonalization, started from the
$J_2=0$ amplitude with Marshall signs, for three amplitude updates:
$a\leftarrow\phi_\FN$; $a\leftarrow\sqrt{a\,\phi_\FN}$, as learned from walkers;
and $a\leftarrow\phi_\FN$ with Anderson extrapolation from the last three iterates.
(a) Sign and (b) energy error vs iteration, compared with ED. All values are
exact (no statistical error).}
\label{fig:closedloop}
\end{figure}

The loop reaches the exact ground state (Fig.~\ref{fig:closedloop}): exact
signs after 100 iterations on $4\times4$ and 224 on the 20-site cluster. The
slowness comes from the amplitude, since the fixed-node Hamiltonian depends on
the guide amplitude through the moved matrix elements. Anderson acceleration
of $\log a$ cuts this to 31 and 59 iterations. The geometric-mean update $a\leftarrow\sqrt{a\,\phi_\FN}$ that walker
learning provides converges as well, exactly twice as slowly (200 iterations).
Exact diagonalization of $H_\FN$ costs as much as ED itself, so this proves
the idea, not its scaling.

\subsection{Sampled fixed node on $4\times4$}
\label{sec:sampled4}

At scale, fixed node returns walkers, not $\phi_\FN$. To isolate the effect
of sampling, we repeat the loop of Sec.~\ref{sec:loop} on $4\times4$ but
estimate $\phi_\FN$ from $N$ samples of the walker distribution
$f\propto a\,\phi_\FN$: on this small cluster every configuration is visited,
so $\phi_\FN(x)\propto{\rm count}(x)/a(x)$ needs no network. Everything else
stays exact.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig6_sampled_4x4.pdf}
\caption{Fixed-node--Krylov loop on $4\times4$ with $\phi_\FN$ estimated from
$N$ independent samples per iteration (mean over four seeds; two for
$N=10^9$), and the exact loop. (a) Sign and (b) energy error vs iteration,
compared with ED.}
\label{fig:sampled}
\end{figure}

Sampling noise sets a floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:sampled}). The energy error
plateaus at $1.3\times10^{-2}$, $1.7\times10^{-3}$ and $2.2\times10^{-4}$ for
$N=10^6$, $10^7$ and $10^8$, so the excess over the exact loop falls roughly
as $1/N$; from $N=10^8$ the signs follow the exact loop. With $N=10^5$ most
configurations go unvisited and the loop degrades. Real fixed-node walkers are
worth fewer samples than independent draws: at $10^6$ and $10^7$ walker
snapshots the loop matches about $3\times10^5$ and $4\times10^6$ independent
samples.

\subsection{First iteration at scale: $6\times6$ and $8\times8$}

One iteration needs no learned amplitude: the guide is the ViT amplitude with
Krylov signs (from Marshall), and fixed node returns its energy directly from
the walkers. We compare it with the ViT itself and with fixed node guided by
the full ViT (its own amplitude and signs), all at $M=128$, $\beta=1.2$, two
walker populations.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig7_guides_compare.pdf}
\caption{One untrained iteration on $6\times6$ and $8\times8$: fixed-node
energy with Krylov or Marshall signs on the ViT amplitude, relative to (a) the
ViT variational energy and (b) fixed node with the full ViT guide.}
\label{fig:guides}
\end{figure}

Krylov signs recover most of the gap between Marshall and the network signs,
from $4\times10^{-3}$ to $6\times10^{-4}$ per site on both lattices, but not
all of it (Fig.~\ref{fig:guides}). Fixed node does not close the remaining gap:
our energies, $-0.502984(18)$ on $6\times6$ and $-0.498210(25)$ on $8\times8$,
lie above the ViT itself, $-0.503600(41)$ and $-0.498823(35)$. Fixed node with
the full ViT guide reproduces the ViT energy within errors, which bounds the
fixed-node bias at this $M$ to about $10^{-4}$ per site.
Figure~\ref{fig:bench} places all values among published results.

\subsection{Closing the loop at scale: learning the amplitude}

On $6\times6$, closing one loop iteration at $M=128$ with a
binned amplitude correction leaves the signs unchanged and gives a next
amplitude correction statistically consistent with zero, with
$E_\FN=-18.1130(36)$ from two populations. Within this resolution the
$6\times6$ loop has reached a fixed point.

To iterate further, the walker information must become a positive amplitude
that can be evaluated on any configuration, i.e. a learned network. Walkers
sample $f\propto a\,\phi_\FN$, so maximum likelihood gives the geometric-mean update
$a_{\rm new}^2\propto a\,\phi_\FN$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:halfstep}. On $8\times8$ we
train the ViT amplitude toward this target with SR, apply a Krylov step, and
rerun fixed node (Fig.~\ref{fig:amplearn}).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{fig9_learning_8x8.pdf}
\caption{$8\times8$ fixed-node energy per site along the learned loop (two
walker populations each). Dotted: best same-geometry literature value
\cite{QianQin2023}.}
\label{fig:amplearn}
\end{figure}

Starting from the untrained result of Sec.~III~D ($-0.49821$ per site, above the ViT), one
learned update gave $-0.49846$, but an independent rebuild of the same update
gave $-0.49748$, and a second update $-0.49711$, both above the start. Keeping
the signs after the first update and changing only the amplitude already
raises the energy, so the learned amplitude, not
the sign step, fails. Learning a callable amplitude accurate enough for fixed
node is the open problem.

\section{Discussion}

Splitting $\psi=s\,a$ separates two problems of different difficulty. The
sign problem, given the amplitude, is easy: a label-free threshold on the
local energy captures the frustrated sign corrections, a few repetitions
reach the exact signs on exact clusters, and on $6\times6$ one step matches
the signs of an optimized neural network energetically. With an approximate
amplitude the same step returns the best signs for that amplitude, so the
residual error is an amplitude error.

The amplitude problem is sign free once the signs are fixed, and lattice fixed
node solves it with an upper-bound guarantee. Alternating both updates exactly
reaches the ground state; the loop is slow because $H_\FN$ depends on the guide
amplitude, and Anderson mixing speeds it up threefold. With sampled fixed node
the loop still converges, but to a floor that falls roughly as the inverse
number of samples; on $4\times4$, $10^7$--$10^8$ samples per iteration are
needed to follow the exact loop, and correlated walkers are worth about three
times fewer.

On $6\times6$ and $8\times8$ one untrained iteration brings the fixed-node
energy within $6\times10^{-4}$ per site of the underlying network but not
below it. Iterating requires a callable amplitude learned from fixed node.
For the energy to decrease with certainty, the learned guide must reach a
variational energy below the previous fixed-node energy, since
$E_\FN[a_{k+1}]\leq\langle H\rangle_{a_{k+1}}$; on $8\times8$ our learned
updates did not, and the energy rose. The consistent objective is the energy
of the network under the frozen fixed-node Hamiltonian, which on exact
$4\times4$ recovers $99.7\%$ of the fixed-node gain; its finite-sample
behaviour is the open question. Fixed node then serves as the referee of each
learned step and supplies the reported energy.

The approach also requires a useful starting amplitude and differs from
repeated Lanczos steps: keeping the Krylov amplitude makes the energy worse
on $6\times6$ (Appendix~\ref{app:k2}).

\appendix

\section{Direct second Krylov step}
\label{app:k2}

On $6\times6$, retaining the full first Krylov amplitude gives
$a_1(x)=|T_1-r_0(x)|a_0(x)$ with coefficient of variation about $0.18$.
A second label-free threshold changes only $0.8$--$2.0\%$ of the
$|\psi_{\rm ViT}|^2$ probability but raises the fixed-amplitude energy by
$0.65$--$0.96$. The feedback construction therefore re-solves the amplitude
before another sign update.

\section{Statistical conventions}
\label{app:stats}

Exact-diagonalization data are deterministic up to eigensolver precision.
For $6\times6$ fixed-amplitude energies we quote between-chain standard
errors from 64 chains. For fixed-node data with two independent walker
populations, error bars denote half the population difference and are
reproducibility scales only; the $6\times6$ Marshall value uses the standard
error over eight populations.
