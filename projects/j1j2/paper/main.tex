\title{Reconstructing frustrated signs from amplitudes with fixed-node and Krylov updates}

\author{A. Otaifi}
\affiliation{Arnold Sommerfeld Center for Theoretical Physics, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit\"at M\"unchen, 80333 M\"unchen, Germany}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We separate the sign and amplitude problems of a frustrated wave function,
$\psi(x)=s(x)a(x)$, in the square-lattice spin-$1/2$ $J_1$--$J_2$
Heisenberg model.  For fixed amplitude, one shifted Krylov step gives
$s'(x)=s(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r_s(x)]$, where
$r_s=(H\psi)/\psi$.  At $J_2/J_1=0.5$ this reduces the exact $4\times4$
wrong-sign weight from $1.27\times10^{-2}$ to $3.41\times10^{-4}$.
On $6\times6$, with the same neural-network amplitude, the reconstructed
signs are energetically indistinguishable from the original neural signs.
Conversely, fixed-node projection supplies a sign-free amplitude update; an
exact $4\times4$ fixed-node/Krylov loop reaches the exact ground-state
signs.  On $8\times8$, the reconstructed signs retain a competitive
fixed-node energy.  The remaining scaling problem is learning a callable
amplitude from fixed-node walkers.  Maximum likelihood targets
$a_{\rm new}\propto\sqrt{a\phi_{\FN}}$, and stochastic
reconfiguration yields an update direction that transfers between
independent walker populations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Frustration produces sign-changing path weights and the Monte Carlo sign
problem \cite{HeneliusSandvik2000,TroyerWiese2005}.  The square-lattice
$J_1$--$J_2$ Heisenberg model is a standard case: the Marshall sign rule is
exact at $J_2=0$ \cite{Marshall1955}, but fails near
$J_2/J_1=1/2$, where accurate variational states are already nontrivial
\cite{Choo2019,ChenHeyl2024}.

We ask whether signs and amplitudes can be improved separately.  Write a
real wave function as
\begin{equation}
\psi(x)=s(x)a(x),\qquad s(x)=\pm1,quad a(x)>0 .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
Given $a$, a single Krylov step changes only $s$.  Given $s$, lattice
fixed node gives a sign-free positive-amplitude problem
\cite{vanBemmel1994}.  One iteration is therefore

We test these two operations separately and then together.  Exact
diagonalization establishes the sign update, $6\times6$ and $8\times8$
calculations test its physical effect, and an exact $4\times4$ loop tests
closure.  The large-system bottleneck is then isolated to the amplitude
handoff from fixed-node walkers.

\section{Model and method}

We study
\begin{equation}
H=J_1\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j
 +J_2\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle}
 \mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j ,
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
on periodic $L\times L$ lattices with $J_1=1$ and $S^z=0$.

\subsection{Krylov sign update}

For $\psi=sa$, define the local energy
\begin{equation}
r_s(x)=\frac{(H\psi)(x)}{\psi(x)} .
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
[(T-H)\psi](x)=[T-r_s(x)]\psi(x),
\end{equation}
the sign of the shifted Krylov vector is
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm K}(x)=s(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r_s(x)] .
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
We keep this sign and discard the Krylov amplitude factor
$|T-r_s|$.  Thus the method is not repeated Lanczos multiplication.
For exact systems, $T$ minimizes the fixed-amplitude energy; on sampled
systems it is obtained from a two-cluster split of the $r_s$ distribution.

When the exact ground state $\psi_0=s_0a_0$ is available, we report
\begin{equation}
O_s=\left|\sum_x a_0(x)^2s(x)s_0(x)\right|,qquad
w_s=\frac{1-O_s}{2},
\end{equation}
where $w_s$ is the exact probability weight carrying the wrong sign.

\subsection{Fixed-node amplitude update}

For guide $\psi_G$, a bond $x\leftrightarrow y$ is sign violating when
$H_{xy}\psi_G(y)/\psi_G(x)>0$.  Lattice fixed node removes these
off-diagonal terms and adds their contribution to the diagonal
\cite{vanBemmel1994}.  The resulting Hamiltonian is sign free after
importance sampling and has the imposed guide signs.

If its positive ground-state modulus is $\phi_{\FN}$, an exact small-system
loop can feed $\phi_{\FN}$ directly into Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}.  In a walker
calculation the stationary mixed distribution is instead
\begin{equation}
f(x)\propto a(x)\phi_{\FN}(x).
\label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
For a Born model $p_\theta(x)\propto a_\theta(x)^2$, exact
maximum likelihood therefore gives
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm new}(x)\propto\sqrt{a(x)\phi_{\FN}(x)} .
\label{eq:halfstep}
\end{equation}

The $4\times4$ exact calculations use the full $S^z=0$ space
($12\,870$ states).  For $6\times6$ and $8\times8$ we use a previously
optimized translationally invariant vision-transformer neural quantum state
(ViT) at $J_2/J_1=0.5$.  The controlled sign tests hold its modulus fixed.
The $8\times8$ fixed-node runs use population $M=128$ and projection length
$\beta=1.2$.

\section{One-step sign reconstruction}

Figure~\ref{fig:signbench}(a,b) uses the exact $4\times4$ ground-state
modulus.  At $J_2/J_1=0.5$, Marshall signs have
$O_s=0.974538$ ($w_s=1.27\times10^{-2}$).  One Krylov update gives
$O_s=0.999319$ ($w_s=3.41\times10^{-4}$) and reduces the
fixed-amplitude energy error per site from $1.16\times10^{-2}$ to
$5.51\times10^{-4}$.  The same construction remains effective across the
sweep when the parent sign pattern is chosen in the appropriate Marshall or
stripe basin.

The $6\times6$ test removes the exact-sign oracle.  Using the same ViT
modulus for all states,
\begin{align}
e_{\rm Marshall}&=-0.4981534,\nonumber\\
e_{\rm Krylov}&=-0.5033337,\nonumber\\
e_{\rm ViT}&=-0.5034504 .
\end{align}
The paired difference $e_{\rm Krylov}-e_{\rm ViT}
=1.17(287)\times10^{-4}$ is unresolved, while Krylov improves over Marshall
by $4.79\times10^{-3}$ per site.  Thus the energy gain comes from the signs,
not from changing the amplitude.

The mechanism is visible in the Marshall local field,
\begin{equation}
r_M=D+F_{J_1}+F_{J_2}.
\end{equation}
The useful signal is mainly the competition between the two off-diagonal
terms.  On independent $6\times6$ samples, the physically weighted wrong-sign
mass falls from $0.01635$ to $8.08\times10^{-4}$ (train) and from $0.01811$
to $8.02\times10^{-4}$ (validation), i.e. about a twenty-fold reduction.

\section{Closing the loop exactly}

We now target $J_2/J_1=0.5$ but initialize with the exact $J_2=0$ modulus
and Marshall signs.  At each step we solve the current fixed-node problem
exactly, replace the amplitude by its positive modulus, and recompute
Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}.  Target-state information is used only for diagnostics.

Figure~\ref{fig:closedloop} shows the result.  The initial amplitude fidelity
is $0.7132$ and the sign overlap is $0.974538$.  After two iterations the sign
overlap is $0.995945$, after twenty it is $0.9999982$, and at iteration 100
the exact sign pattern is reached.  The final amplitude fidelity is
$0.9999946$ and the guide energy is $2.60\times10^{-5}$ above the exact
ground state.

The plateaus are physical: fixed node can improve the amplitude for many
steps without changing a sign.  A sign update occurs only when the improved
amplitude shifts the local-energy threshold across a new set of
configurations.

\section{Scaling to $8\times8$}

At $M=128$, the two-population means are
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm K-FN}=-31.88542,\qquad
E_{\rm M-FN}=-31.67748 .
\end{equation}
Their difference is $-0.20795$.  Two-population spreads are used only as
reproducibility scales.

The Krylov-sign mean is $E/N=-0.498210$, compared with $-0.494961$ for
Marshall signs.  Figure~\ref{fig:8x8bench} shows that it lies in the same
energy range as high-accuracy DMRG/FAMPS and neural benchmarks on the same
geometry.  We use this only as a scale comparison, not as a ranking between
methods.

The sign update therefore survives to $8\times8$.  The bottleneck is now
different: fixed-node walkers must be converted into an amplitude that can be
evaluated on arbitrary configurations.

\section{Learning the amplitude from walkers}

Equation~\eqref{eq:halfstep} can be tested exactly on $4\times4$.
Starting from the exact target modulus with Marshall signs, one ideal
half-step plus the Krylov sign update changes the energy from $-8.27291$ to
$-8.44579$, close to $E_0=-8.45792$.  A uniform-amplitude start is initially
nonmonotone but subsequently recovers [Fig.~\ref{fig:amplearn}(a)].

At $8\times8$, the issue is sampling noise.  Let $g_1$ and $g_2$ be
maximum-likelihood gradients from two independent fixed-node populations.
Their Euclidean cosine is
\begin{equation}
\frac{g_1\cdot g_2}{\|g_1\|\|g_2\|}=-0.853,
\end{equation}
so an ordinary gradient step does not transfer between populations.

We therefore use stochastic reconfiguration (SR)
\cite{Sorella2001},
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta=(S+\lambda I)^{-1}g_1 .
\end{equation}
For $\lambda=1$, the held-out directional derivative becomes
$g_2^{\mathsf T}\delta\theta=+3.85$.  The finite line search in
Fig.~\ref{fig:amplearn}(b) remains positive and peaks near $\eta=0.01$,
where the held-out log-likelihood gain is $0.0156$ and the reweighting
effective sample size is $3236/4096$.

This establishes a transferable amplitude-learning direction.  It does not
yet establish convergence of a repeatedly learned $8\times8$ loop; fixed-node
energy remains the physical acceptance test.

\section{Discussion and outlook}

The data point to a simple division of labor.  A good amplitude makes the
dominant frustrated sign correction almost one-dimensional: a threshold in
the local Krylov field.  Fixed node solves the complementary positive-amplitude
problem at fixed signs, and the exact $4\times4$ loop shows that the two
updates can close.

The unresolved large-system problem is the amplitude handoff from projector
walkers.  SR makes one $8\times8$ update transferable across independent
populations, but repeated updates still need fixed-node energy validation.
The method also requires a useful initial amplitude and should not be confused
with repeated bare Krylov iteration; the direct second step is worse on
$6\times6$.

\section{Direct second Krylov step}

On $6\times6$, retaining the full first Krylov amplitude gives
$a_1(x)=|T_1-r_0(x)|a_0(x)$ with coefficient of variation about $0.18$.
A second unsupervised threshold changes only $0.8$--$2.0\%$ of physical
weight but raises the fixed-amplitude energy by $0.65$--$0.96$ in total
energy.  The feedback construction therefore restores or re-solves the
amplitude before another sign update.

\section{Statistical convention}

Exact-diagonalization data are deterministic up to eigensolver precision.
For the $6\times6$ fixed-amplitude comparison we use correlation-robust
between-chain standard errors.  For $8\times8$ fixed-node data with two
independent populations, error bars denote half the population difference and
are treated only as reproducibility scales.
