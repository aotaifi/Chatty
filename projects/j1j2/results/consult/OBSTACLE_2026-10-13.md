# The obstacle, stated precisely (2026-10-13) — for independent brainstorms

Goal: an iterative improvement loop for a variational state psi of the J1-J2 Heisenberg model (square lattice, J2/J1 = 0.5,
near-critical, frustrated) whose per-iteration cost does NOT grow with the iteration count k, so that many
Lanczos/imaginary-time-quality steps can be applied at a cost far below p explicit Lanczos steps ((2.3N)^p network
evaluations per sample, N sites).

Each iteration k: from the current guide psi_k compute a target psi_{k+1} = F(psi_k) where F is local in the Hamiltonian
graph (one "hop": needs psi_k at the ~2.3N configurations reachable by one H move), e.g. a clipped Lanczos factor
(1 + alpha(E_L - E)), an FN/imaginary-time step, or a sign flip; then STORE psi_{k+1} in a representation that can be
evaluated at fixed cost (independent of k), so the next iteration again needs one hop.

Measured facts (exact 6x6 tables with 15.8M orbit representatives; 8x8 two-hop sample data):
1. The one-step update is learned to ~84-100% by a small network that receives the CURRENT guide's own one-hop
   quantities (local energy E_L, violating/kept neighbour sums V, W).
2. Storing it at fixed cost fails from iteration 2 for every representation tried:
   - small nets on the fixed base's one-hop quantities: keep 2-31% (99% if given the current guide's E_L = a 2nd hop);
   - spin-only networks (fine-tuned ViT, additive spin-only tail nets): precise (0.003-0.006 in bond log-ratio) on
     configurations with |psi|^2 > 1e-8, wrong (0.1-0.9) on |psi|^2 < 1e-10, where the gain of a step sits
     (newly flipped / near-node configurations, ~8% of the weight at 6x6); splices: exact tail + net elsewhere keeps 91%;
   - a fixed one-hop Lanczos wrapper (1 + beta(H - E)) N: tails of earlier steps are not regenerated (inheritance
     dominates only at newly flipped configurations);
   - adaptive depth (2nd hop only at near-node flagged configurations, 2% of weight): recovers ~78% but the flagged
     region branches with ~50 flagged neighbours per flagged configuration;
   - input-gradient (linearised) neighbour ratios: useless (one swap is far outside the linear regime).
3. Physics: the double-swap non-additivity I(b,b') = la(x^{bb'}) + la(x) - la(x^b) - la(x^{b'}) of the ground state is
   O(0.3-0.7) rms at all accessible distances at J2 = 0.5 (6x6 and 8x8; exact state = ViT), also for the residual
   relative to PP, RBM+PP or ViT bases (heavy-tailed: large when a configuration lands near the base's nodes); local
   at J2 = 0, nearly at 0.3, non-local at 0.4-0.6 (4x6 exact).
4. On the lattice, the fixed-node energy of a guide depends on its amplitude at the nodes (wall term
   sum_bad |H_xy| a(y)/a(x)): same sign, E_FN 1.95e-5 (exact amplitude) vs 9.2e-5 (fitted ViT) per site.
5. Storing a SIGN step works at fixed cost (8x8: sign network + one exact hop beats two exact Krylov steps from the
   Marshall sign). Global (non-local) steps are not diluted below ~1e3-1e4 sites for ViT-quality guides.

Question: how can the loop store its improvements at fixed per-iteration cost despite 2-4? What assumption shared by
all failed designs could be dropped (what is stored, what is iterated, what objective, what representation, what
is evaluated at the end)? Ideas must respect: final energies variational (or FN upper bounds) or with controlled bias;
cost compared honestly with p Lanczos steps and with brute-force NQS training.
