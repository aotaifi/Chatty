# Frustration dependence / K1 reconstructibility verdict — 2026-09-30

## Question
Is the exceptional one-step Krylov sign reconstruction near J2/J1=0.5 a point-specific accident, or part of a broader frustration-dependent mechanism?

## Exact 4x4 result
Using exact ground-state amplitudes and fully label-free fixed-amplitude energy optimization of the threshold:

- J2=0.40, Marshall: O 0.999128 -> 0.999983; ~98.1% of wrong sign mass removed.
- J2=0.50, Marshall: O 0.974538 -> 0.999319; ~97.3% removed.
- J2=0.60, Marshall: O 0.795572 -> 0.957131; ~79.0% removed.
- J2=0.80, stripe: O 0.899845 -> 0.975927; ~76.0% removed.
- J2=1.00, stripe: O 0.955819 -> 0.994884; ~88.4% removed.

Thus the one-step mechanism is not unique to J2=0.5. It works broadly when the starting sign reference belongs to the appropriate sign basin.

## Crossover region
Dense exact scan:

J2=0.62:
- Marshall O 0.7043 -> 0.6814 (one step worsens)
- stripe O 0.6084 -> 0.8374
- iterated exact-amplitude map: Marshall exact in 6 steps, stripe exact in 5.

J2=0.64:
- Marshall O 0.5897 -> 0.5527 (one step worsens)
- stripe O 0.6557 -> 0.8456
- iterated map: Marshall exact in 6, stripe exact in 5.

J2=0.66:
- Marshall O 0.4710 -> 0.3986 and converges to a wrong fixed point.
- stripe O 0.7075 -> 0.8065 and reaches exact signs in 4.

Refined basin boundary:
- J2=0.645: Marshall exact in 5; stripe exact in 5.
- J2=0.650: Marshall exact in 7; stripe exact in 5.
- J2=0.655: Marshall reaches a wrong fixed point; stripe exact in 5.

Hence there is a finite coexistence interval where both parent gauges are in the attraction basin, followed by a nonlinear basin switch between roughly 0.650 and 0.655 on 4x4.

## Important negative result
Choosing the starting parent gauge by its raw fixed-amplitude energy, or even selecting the lowest post-K1 fixed-amplitude energy, does NOT always select the sign reference with the best hidden-sign overlap in the crossover. Energy and sign overlap decouple there.

So there is no universal rule:
"choose the lower-energy simple gauge and one K1 step reconstructs the signs."

## Mechanism
Frustration dependence mainly changes the geometry of the sign basin.

Away from the crossover:
- the exact sign structure is a relatively sparse correction to a simple parent gauge (Marshall or stripe);
- the amplitude-weighted local-energy / off-diagonal frustration field separates those defects well;
- one K1-like projected step removes most physical wrong-sign mass.

Near the crossover:
- the target sign structure is genuinely intermediate between parent gauges;
- one-step threshold compression can worsen sign overlap;
- nevertheless the iterated projected-Krylov map can still converge exactly while the starting gauge remains in the correct nonlinear basin.

Beyond the basin boundary a wrong parent gauge can flow to a wrong fixed point, while the appropriate competing gauge still converges.

Crucially, this square-lattice basin switch is **not** the symmetry-sector obstruction identified in the separate basin-invariant work. On the audited square 4x4 cases, the exact ground state, Marshall gauge, and stripe gauges share the compatible translation characters. Therefore the loss of Marshall convergence near J2≈0.655 is a genuine nonlinear attraction-basin transition inside the same symmetry sector, not a symmetry-forbidden flow.

This also means that a poor one-step result is not by itself evidence that the iterative FN/amplitude-refresh/K1 mechanism has failed: near the crossover, one-step overlap can decrease while repeated projected updates still recover the exact node.

## Verdict
The high-ROI structural statement is:

**K1 reconstructibility is controlled more by sign-basin membership and distance from a parent gauge than by frustration J2 itself.**

One-step K1 is a sparse-defect correction inside a basin. The recursive FN/amplitude-refresh/K1 loop is the more general mechanism and can survive where the one-step approximation fails.

This angle is conceptually resolved on exact 4x4. Size dependence / 8x8 validation should be folded into the main scaling loop rather than treated as a separate production campaign.
