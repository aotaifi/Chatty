\title{Projected-Krylov reconstruction of frustrated signs with fixed-node amplitude feedback}

\author{A. Otaifi}
\affiliation{Arnold Sommerfeld Center for Theoretical Physics, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit\"at M\"unchen, 80333 M\"unchen, Germany}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We study the sign structure of the spin-$1/2$ square-lattice $J_1$--$J_2$
Heisenberg model near maximal frustration, focusing on $J_2/J_1=1/2$.
Writing the wave function as $\psi(x)=s(x)a(x)$ with $a(x)>0$, we separate
the reconstruction problem into a sign update and a positive-amplitude update.
A single projected Krylov step provides an accurate, label-free reconstruction
of the physically important sign corrections when supplied with a sufficiently
accurate amplitude, while a lattice fixed-node projector supplies sign-free
information about the positive amplitude for fixed signs.

On exact $4\times4$ systems the coupled fixed-node/Krylov map can recover the
exact ground-state signs without target-ground-state information entering the
updates. On $6\times6$ and $8\times8$, one-step Krylov signs substantially
improve fixed-node energies over Marshall signs and remain competitive with
high-accuracy variational benchmarks. The principal scaling bottleneck is not
the sign rule itself but the handoff from fixed-node walkers to a callable
positive amplitude. We show that independent Euclidean maximum-likelihood
gradients from fixed-node replicas can be strongly anti-aligned, whereas a
matrix-free stochastic-reconfiguration update rotates the direction into one
that improves an independent held-out replica. Repeated learned updates remain
non-monotone in the physical fixed-node objective, motivating a physically
gated outer feedback loop.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Frustration in the square-lattice $J_1$--$J_2$ Heisenberg antiferromagnet
produces a nontrivial many-body sign structure precisely where unbiased
Monte Carlo methods are most difficult to apply. Instead of attempting to
remove the sign structure globally, we ask whether its two components can be
treated by different numerical mechanisms:
\begin{equation}
    \psi(x)=s(x)a(x),\qquad s(x)=\pm1,\quad a(x)>0.
\end{equation}
The central separation in this work is that the sign $s$ is updated by a
projected Krylov rule, whereas positive-amplitude information is supplied by
a sign-free fixed-node projection.

This leads to the feedback architecture
\begin{equation}
s_k
\xrightarrow{\FN}
a_{k+1}
\xrightarrow{\Kone}
s_{k+1},
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}
where the first arrow denotes a positive fixed-node solve at fixed signs and
the second denotes a sign-changing projected-Krylov update. The two arrows
must not be conflated: Krylov reconstruction supplies signs, not the new
positive amplitude.

The paper is organized around three questions. First, how much of the
frustrated sign structure is captured by one Krylov step? Second, does the
coupled fixed-node/Krylov map self-correct on controlled systems? Third, what
prevents the same feedback from scaling to larger lattices?

\section{Projected-Krylov reconstruction of the sign structure}

For a positive amplitude $a(x)$ and a reference sign pattern $s(x)$, define
the local Krylov field
\begin{equation}
 r_s(x)=\frac{H[a s](x)}{a(x)s(x)}.
 \label{eq:localfield}
\end{equation}
A one-step Krylov vector obeys
\begin{equation}
[(T-H)as](x)=[T-r_s(x)]a(x)s(x),
\end{equation}
so retaining only its sign gives
\begin{equation}
s'(x)=s(x)\,\mathrm{sgn}[T-r_s(x)].
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
The threshold $T$ is inferred without target sign labels, using either
fixed-amplitude energy minimization on exact small systems or robust
two-cluster thresholding on sampled larger systems.

Figure~\ref{fig:k1exact} shows the exact $4\times4$ result. At
$J_2/J_1=0.5$, Marshall signs have $\OS=0.974538$, while one K1 update reaches
$\OS=0.999319$. The same step reduces the fixed-amplitude energy error per
site from $1.16\times10^{-2}$ to $5.51\times10^{-4}$. The one-step
reconstruction remains effective over a broad frustration range provided the
reference sign convention lies in the appropriate sign basin.

On $6\times6$, an independent fixed-amplitude comparison using the same ViT
modulus gives
\begin{align}
E_{\rm K1}-E_{\rm ViT}&=+0.00420(1035),\\
E_{\rm K1}-E_{\rm M}&=-0.17247(4489),
\end{align}
in total energy. Thus K1 does not resolve below the ViT sign energy at this
precision, but it reproduces essentially the same physical energy while
substantially improving over Marshall signs.

The useful signal is primarily off diagonal. In the Marshall gauge the
nearest-neighbor $J_1$ exchange contributes with the unfrustrated sign, while
the frustrating $J_2$ exchange contributes oppositely. Their
amplitude-weighted competition produces most of the observed K1 separation.
On the $6\times6$ diagnostic sample, one K1 step removes approximately
$95\%$ of the physically weighted wrong-sign mass.

A naive second Krylov step does not continue this improvement. With the
first-step amplitude distortion left in place, unsupervised K2 thresholds
change only $\sim1$--$2\%$ of physical configurations yet raise the
fixed-amplitude energy by $0.65$--$0.96$ in total energy. We therefore use K1
as the sign reconstruction primitive and obtain subsequent amplitude
information from fixed node rather than from an undamped Krylov continuation.

\section{Fixed-node amplitude feedback}
For fixed signs, the lattice fixed-node Hamiltonian is sign free and its
projected ground state provides a positive amplitude $\phi_{\FN}(x)$.
The desired feedback is therefore Eq.~\eqref{eq:loop}, with each K1 field
recomputed from the current amplitude and current sign state.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{fig3_exact_closed_loop.png}
\caption{Exact $4\times4$ current-sign fixed-node/K1 loop at
$J_2/J_1=0.5$, initialized with Marshall signs and the exact $J_2=0$
ground-state modulus. Target-$J_2$ exact information is used only for the
plotted diagnostics. Both the sign error and amplitude infidelity contract,
with long plateaus followed by discrete sign-chamber changes.}
\label{fig:closed4}
\end{figure*}

The exact $4\times4$ benchmark in Fig.~\ref{fig:closed4} starts from the
$J_2=0$ modulus and Marshall signs. No target-$J_2$ ground-state information
enters either the fixed-node solve or the threshold update. The loop reaches
the exact target sign pattern at iteration 100, with final amplitude fidelity
$F_a=0.9999946$ and guide-energy error $2.60\times10^{-5}$.

This exact result also clarifies an earlier apparent recurrence. A
Marshall-referenced update repeatedly reconstructs a correction in a fixed
coordinate and can stall near $\OS\simeq0.9994$. The genuine nonlinear map
must instead recompute $r_{s_k}$ from the current sign state. In that
formulation long plateaus are allowed: fixed node can improve the amplitude
for many iterations before the next sign chamber becomes energetically
accessible.

\subsection{Mixed walkers and the ideal half step}
Importance-sampled fixed-node walkers obey
\begin{equation}
f(x)\propto a_g(x)\phi_{\FN}(x).
\end{equation}
If a Born model is trained by exact maximum likelihood,
$p_\theta(x)\propto a_\theta(x)^2$, matching $p_\theta=f$ gives
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm new}(x)\propto\sqrt{a_g(x)\phi_{\FN}(x)}.
\label{eq:halfstep}
\end{equation}

Exact $4\times4$ tests verify this geometric-mean half step. Starting from
the exact target modulus with Marshall signs, the first half step followed by
K1 reaches $E=-8.4457866$ from an initial guide energy $-8.2729091$ and has
sign overlap $0.9994168$. A deliberately poor uniform-amplitude start is
non-monotone at the first update but subsequently recovers, showing that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:halfstep} is a local amplitude mechanism rather than a global
monotonicity theorem.

\section{Scaling and the amplitude-handoff bottleneck}

A raw walker histogram is not a scalable callable amplitude. On exact
$4\times4$, millions of ideal independent samples are already required to
approach the exact-amplitude K1 ceiling when each configuration amplitude is
estimated independently. A four-bin density-ratio correction provides a
controlled low-dimensional prototype and stabilizes the $4\times4$ loop
with $\mathcal{O}(10^4)$ samples.

At $6\times6$, increasing the fixed-node population removes the dominant
replica disagreement and the tested four-bin loop reaches a practical fixed
point: after the first amplitude handoff the K1 sign guide is unchanged on
the physical diagnostic measure and the next fitted correction is an order
of magnitude smaller than its pairwise noise. The same static construction
is not computationally viable at $8\times8$, where recomputing nested local
energies generates tens of millions of cached states and hundreds of
millions of ViT evaluations. This identifies the principal scalable object:
a flat callable model for the positive amplitude.

The $8\times8$ K1-FN mean is $E/N=-0.498210$ from two $M=128$ replicas,
compared with $-0.494961$ for Marshall-FN. On the identical $8\times8$
periodic geometry, this places the K1-guided fixed-node result below the
reported DMRG $D=32000$ and FAMPS $D=12000$ energies, while remaining above
the RBM+PP and FAMPS $D=24000$ benchmarks in Ref.~\cite{QianQin2023}.
Because the fixed-node estimate is itself population sampled, this comparison
is used as a scale benchmark rather than a claim of precision ordering among
methods.

\section{Scalable amplitude learning by stochastic reconfiguration}

Direct warm-start maximum-likelihood training of the full ViT amplitude does
not transfer between independent fixed-node replicas under ordinary
Euclidean optimization. At the initial $8\times8$ guide, the two empirical
MLE gradients have
\begin{equation}
\cos(g_1,g_2)=-0.8529.
\end{equation}
This anti-alignment persists in an infinitesimal Euclidean line search and is
therefore not merely an oversized Adam step.

Matrix-free stochastic reconfiguration changes the geometry. With empirical
Fisher matrix $S$ and diagonal shift $\lambda=1$,
\begin{equation}
g_2^T(S+\lambda I)^{-1}g_1=+3.85,
\end{equation}
despite the negative Euclidean cosine. A finite line search then gives a
held-out log-likelihood gain $+0.015575$ at $\eta=0.01$, while retaining an
importance-sampling ESS of $3236/4096$.

\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig4_sr_learning.png}
\caption{Cross-replica amplitude-learning diagnostics on $8\times8$.
Left: Euclidean gradient cosine for the first and second learned updates.
Right: held-out likelihood gain along the matrix-free SR direction at
$\lambda=1$. The validation-selected steps are $\eta=0.01$ and $0.003$.}

The corresponding amplitude refresh shifts the label-free K1 threshold from
$T_0=-28.37108$ to $T_1=-28.82417$. Under the correct
$a^{1.2}$ threshold measure, the K1 branch changes on $9.27\%$ of the
reweighted mass. Thus the learned amplitude update is not merely a
likelihood perturbation; it generates a nontrivial sign refresh.

\section{Closed-loop convergence and present limitation}

A first pair of matched $M=128$ fixed-node runs using the SR-updated amplitude
and refreshed K1 signs gave total energies $-31.89679$ and $-31.90567$.
However, an independent reconstruction of a numerically indistinguishable SR
checkpoint produced a second pair with mean $-31.83896$, despite log-amplitude
differences below $5\times10^{-6}$ on a direct state audit. We therefore do
not use the first pair alone as evidence for a statistically resolved energy
improvement over the baseline K1-FN value $-31.88542$.

The second learned SR step provides a complementary convergence diagnostic.
Its independent-replica Euclidean gradients become aligned,
$\cos(g_1,g_2)=+0.8568$, and the validation-optimal SR step shrinks to
$\eta=0.003$. Nevertheless, after amplitude refresh and K1 reconstruction,
the matched FN mean worsens to
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm iter2}=-31.81537,
\end{equation}
with a two-replica spread proxy $0.00947$. Improving the MLE objective is
therefore not sufficient to accept an outer-loop update.

A frozen-sign diagnostic isolates this failure mode by holding the successful
iteration-1 signs $s_1$ fixed while replacing $a_1$ by the iteration-2
amplitude $a_2$. The first completed replica gives
$E_{\FN}[a_2,s_1]=-31.84646$, already worse than the corresponding
$E_{\FN}[a_1,s_1]=-31.89679$. The paired frozen-sign run is still in
progress; the interpretation will be updated only after that control closes.

\section{Discussion}

The results support a clean division of labor. One projected Krylov step is
a cheap extractor of the physically important frustrated sign structure.
Fixed node supplies positive-amplitude information without reintroducing a
sign problem. Their exact $4\times4$ composition is self-correcting, and the
K1 sign guide remains effective through $8\times8$.

The unresolved scaling problem is the amplitude handoff. Static
nonparametric or nested corrections become computationally expensive, while
ordinary Euclidean neural updates are replica dependent. Matrix-free
stochastic reconfiguration supplies the first transferable learned direction
at $8\times8$, but the physical projector must remain the acceptance
criterion for repeated updates.

The natural next step is therefore not a more complicated sign model. It is
a statistically controlled outer loop in which each learned amplitude step is
validated on independent FN populations and accepted only if a matched
fixed-node energy diagnostic is stable under replication. The current
frozen-sign experiment further separates amplitude degradation from any
failure of K1 itself.
