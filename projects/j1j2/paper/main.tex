\title{Reconstructing frustrated signs from amplitudes with fixed-node and Krylov updates}

\author{A. Otaifi}
\affiliation{Arnold Sommerfeld Center for Theoretical Physics, Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit\"at M\"unchen, 80333 M\"unchen, Germany}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We separate the sign and positive-amplitude parts of a real many-body wave
function, $\psi(x)=s(x)a(x)$ with $a(x)=|\psi(x)|>0$, in the frustrated
square-lattice spin-$1/2$ $J_1$--$J_2$ Heisenberg model. For a fixed
amplitude, one shifted Krylov step gives
$s'(x)=s(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r_s(x)]$, where
$r_s(x)=(H\psi)(x)/\psi(x)$. At $J_2/J_1=0.5$ this reduces the exact
$4\times4$ probability weight on incorrectly signed configurations from
$1.27\times10^{-2}$ to $3.41\times10^{-4}$. On $6\times6$, using exactly
the same neural-network amplitude, the reconstructed signs give the same
energy as the original neural-network signs within sampling uncertainty.
Conversely, lattice fixed-node projection improves the positive amplitude
while keeping the signs fixed; an exact $4\times4$ fixed-node/Krylov loop
reaches the exact ground-state signs. The reconstructed signs remain useful
on $8\times8$. The remaining scaling problem is to infer from fixed-node
Monte Carlo walkers a positive amplitude function that can be evaluated on
arbitrary configurations. Maximum likelihood targets
$a_{\rm new}^2\propto a\phi_{\FN}$; stochastic reconfiguration makes this
update transfer between independent walker samples, but repeated $8\times8$
iterations do not yet lower the fixed-node energy reproducibly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Monte Carlo sign problem is severe in frustrated spin systems
\cite{HeneliusSandvik2000,TroyerWiese2005}. We use the square-lattice
$J_1$--$J_2$ Heisenberg model as a benchmark. At $J_2=0$ its ground-state
signs obey the Marshall rule \cite{Marshall1955}; near $J_2/J_1=1/2$ that
rule fails and accurate variational states become nontrivial
\cite{Choo2019,ChenHeyl2024}.

We ask whether signs and positive amplitudes can be improved separately.
For a real wave function we write
\begin{equation}
\psi(x)=s(x)a(x),\qquad s(x)=\pm1,\quad a(x)=|\psi(x)|>0 .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
Given $a$, a single Krylov step updates the signs. Given $s$, lattice fixed
node gives a sign-free positive-amplitude problem \cite{vanBemmel1994}.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines both updates. Section~\ref{sec:results}
tests them in order of increasing difficulty: first the sign update alone
with an exact amplitude, then the exact feedback loop, then larger lattices,
and finally the open problem of learning the amplitude from fixed-node
walkers.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Model and error measures}

We study
\begin{equation}
H=J_1\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j
 +J_2\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle}
 \mathbf S_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf S_j ,
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
on periodic lattices with $J_1=1$ in the $S^z=0$ sector. For a spin
configuration $x$, the Marshall rule is $s_M(x)=(-1)^{N^A_\downarrow(x)}$ up
to an overall sign, where $N^A_\downarrow$ counts down spins on one
checkerboard sublattice.

When the exact ground state $\psi_0=s_0a_0$ is available, we fix the
irrelevant overall sign and measure the sign error by the wrong-sign
probability
\begin{equation}
w_s=\sum_{x:\,s(x)\neq s_0(x)} a_0(x)^2 ,
\label{eq:signweight}
\end{equation}
and the energy error by $\epsilon_{\rm rel}=(E-E_0)/|E_0|$.

\subsection{One-step Krylov sign update}

For $\psi=sa$, define the local energy
\begin{equation}
r_s(x)=\frac{(H\psi)(x)}{\psi(x)} .
\label{eq:localenergy}
\end{equation}
Because $[(T-H)\psi](x)=[T-r_s(x)]\psi(x)$, the sign of the shifted Krylov
vector is
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm K}(x)=s(x)\operatorname{sgn}[T-r_s(x)] :
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
the sign of every configuration with $r_s(x)>T$ is flipped. Only this sign
is kept; the amplitude factor $|T-r_s(x)|$ is discarded, so the method is not
repeated Lanczos multiplication. The threshold $T$ never uses the target
signs. For exact systems, $T$ minimizes the variational energy of
$a(x)s_{\rm K}(x)$ over all thresholds. For sampled systems, $T$ is obtained
from a two-cluster split of the sampled $r_s(x)$ values.

\subsection{Fixed-node amplitude update}

For a trial wave function $\psi_T$, an off-diagonal matrix element connecting
$x$ and $y$ is sign violating when $H_{xy}\psi_T(y)/\psi_T(x)>0$. Lattice
fixed node removes such terms and adds their contribution to the diagonal
\cite{vanBemmel1994}. The resulting Hamiltonian is sign free, keeps the
signs of $\psi_T$ fixed, and its ground-state energy $E_\FN$ is a variational
upper bound. Let $\phi_\FN(x)>0$ be its positive ground-state amplitude. In an
exact small-system calculation, $\phi_\FN$ replaces $a$ directly before the
next application of Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}.

\subsection{Learning the amplitude from walkers}

In a walker calculation $\phi_\FN$ is not available as a function. The
stationary walker distribution is instead the mixed distribution
\begin{equation}
f(x)\propto a(x)\phi_\FN(x) .
\label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
If a positive model is trained by maximum likelihood with
$p_\theta(x)\propto a_\theta(x)^2$, the exact maximum-likelihood target is
\begin{equation}
a_{\rm new}^2(x)\propto a(x)\phi_\FN(x),
\label{eq:halfstep}
\end{equation}
the geometric mean of the current and fixed-node amplitudes. We
precondition the likelihood gradient $g$ with stochastic reconfiguration
(SR),
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta=(S+\lambda I)^{-1}g ,
\label{eq:sr}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the quantum geometric tensor of $\log a_\theta$.

\subsection{Numerical setup}

The $4\times4$ calculations use the full $S^z=0$ space of $12\,870$ states;
the second exact cluster is an $N=20$ skew torus. For $6\times6$ and $8\times8$
we use a previously optimized translationally invariant vision-transformer
neural quantum state (ViT) at $J_2/J_1=0.5$. Fixed-node energies are mixed
estimates from walker populations of size $M$ and projection length
$\beta=1.2$; $6\times6$ uses $M=32$ and $M=128$, $8\times8$ uses $M=128$.
Statistical conventions are summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:stats}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Benchmark 1: exact amplitude, Marshall signs, one Krylov step}

The first test isolates the sign update. We take the exact $4\times4$
ground-state amplitude $a_0=|\psi_0|$ from exact diagonalization and give it
the Marshall signs, so the guide $\psi(x)=s_M(x)a_0(x)$ has a perfect
amplitude and only its signs are wrong. For $J_2/J_1\geq0.8$, where the
Marshall pattern is the wrong reference, we use the corresponding stripe
sign rule instead. We apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1} once and compare the result
with exact diagonalization.

Figure~\ref{fig:proof}(a) shows why one step works at $J_2/J_1=0.5$. The
configurations on which the Marshall sign is wrong carry systematically
larger local energies $r(x)$ than the correctly signed ones, and the
energy-optimal threshold $T$ separates the two groups: $98\%$ of the
wrong-sign probability lies at $r>T$. The wrong-sign probability therefore
drops from $1.27\times10^{-2}$ to $3.41\times10^{-4}$, a factor $37$, and
the relative energy error from $2.19\times10^{-2}$ to $1.04\times10^{-3}$, a
factor $21$. The improvement holds across the frustrated regime
[Fig.~\ref{fig:proof}(b,c)] and on the independent $N=20$ cluster, where the
factors are $50$ and $24$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:proof}(d)].

\subsection{Exact fixed-node--Krylov feedback}

Next both parts are updated. We target $J_2/J_1=0.5$ but start from the exact
$J_2=0$ amplitude with Marshall signs. Each iteration diagonalizes the
current fixed-node Hamiltonian exactly, takes its positive ground state
$\phi_\FN$ as the new amplitude, and recomputes the signs with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1}. No ground-state information enters either update; exact
diagonalization of $H$ is used only to evaluate the errors.

On $N=16$ the wrong-sign probability falls from $1.27\times10^{-2}$ to
$8.85\times10^{-7}$ after twenty iterations and to zero within numerical
precision by iteration 100, where the amplitude fidelity is $0.9999946$ and
$\epsilon_{\rm rel}=3.08\times10^{-6}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:closedloop}). The
$N=20$ cluster behaves the same way: after 40 iterations $w_s=2.55\times10^{-7}$,
$1-F_a=8.15\times10^{-5}$, and $\epsilon_{\rm rel}=1.41\times10^{-5}$.

\subsection{$6\times6$: neural amplitude without an exact reference}

On $6\times6$ we keep the ViT positive amplitude fixed and compare three sign
choices: Marshall, one Krylov step from Marshall, and the original ViT signs.
The ViT signs are a reference, not the exact ground state. One Krylov step
reduces the $|\psi_{\rm ViT}|^2$-weighted disagreement with the ViT signs by
a factor of about $20$ on two independent samples
[Fig.~\ref{fig:larger}(a)]. With the same amplitude, the energies per site are
$e_{\rm Marshall}=-0.4982(16)$ and $e_{\rm ViT}=-0.50345(16)$. Paired
estimators on identical configurations give
$e_{\rm Krylov}-e_{\rm ViT}=(1.2\pm2.9)\times10^{-4}$, statistically zero, and
$e_{\rm Krylov}-e_{\rm Marshall}=(-4.8\pm1.2)\times10^{-3}$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:larger}(b)].

The Krylov signs also improve fixed node. In a matched $M=32$ comparison the
Krylov-sign fixed-node energy is $E_\FN=-18.128$ ($-0.50356$ per site),
against $-17.973(30)$ ($-0.49924$ per site) for Marshall signs averaged over
eight independent populations. Closing one loop iteration at $M=128$ with a
binned amplitude correction leaves the signs unchanged and gives a next
amplitude correction statistically consistent with zero, with
$E_\FN=-18.1130(36)$ from two populations. Within this resolution the
$6\times6$ loop has reached a fixed point.

\subsection{$8\times8$: fixed node with Krylov signs}

On $8\times8$ the Krylov signs are applied to the ViT amplitude and used as
the fixed-node guide. At $M=128$ two independent populations give
\begin{align}
E_\FN^{\rm Krylov}&=-31.8854\quad(E/N=-0.498210),\nonumber\\
E_\FN^{\rm Marshall}&=-31.6775\quad(E/N=-0.494961).
\end{align}
The two Krylov populations differ by $5\times10^{-5}$ per site, the Marshall
populations by $8\times10^{-4}$. Figure~\ref{fig:8x8bench} compares these
values with same-geometry results from Ref.~\cite{QianQin2023}. The
Krylov-sign value lies among the most accurate published estimates. Because
only two populations at a single $M$ are available, the population spread is a
reproducibility scale, not an estimate of population-control bias.

\subsection{Open problem: learning the fixed-node amplitude from walkers}

To iterate the loop beyond exact systems, the walker information must be
turned into a positive amplitude that can be evaluated on any configuration.
Equation~\eqref{eq:halfstep} gives the target. On exact $4\times4$, repeated
half steps toward it steadily lower the energy, starting from either the
exact amplitude or a uniform amplitude with Marshall signs [Fig.~\ref{fig:amplearn}(a)].

On $8\times8$ the difficulty is statistical. The plain likelihood gradients
from two independent walker populations have cosine $-0.85$, so a gradient
step fitted to one population worsens the other. With SR, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sr}
with $\lambda=1$, the update fitted to one population raises the likelihood
of the independent population for all step sizes up to $\eta=0.01$, where the
held-out gain is $+0.0156$ with importance-sampling effective sample size
$3236/4096$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:amplearn}(b)].

A transferable likelihood step is not yet an energy improvement. One SR
iteration followed by a new Krylov step gave $E_\FN=-31.9012$ on $8\times8$,
but an independent rebuild of the same update gave $-31.839$, and a second
iteration gave $-31.815$, above the starting $-31.885$. The $8\times8$ loop
therefore does not yet converge. On exact $4\times4$, minimizing the frozen
fixed-node energy with SR in a factorized network
$a_\theta=a_k\exp[r_\theta]$ recovers $99.7\%$ of the exact fixed-node energy
gain, so the network can represent the update; the remaining question is the
finite-sample estimator at scale.

\section{Discussion}

The results separate the two tasks. With a good positive amplitude, the
dominant frustrated sign correction is captured by a single threshold in the
local energy, reducing the exact sign error by factors of $37$--$50$. Fixed
node solves the complementary positive-amplitude problem at fixed signs, and
on exact clusters the two updates close into a loop that reaches the exact
ground state. On $6\times6$ and $8\times8$ the Krylov signs reproduce the
neural-network signs energetically and give competitive fixed-node energies.

The open problem is the amplitude step at scale: learning a callable
positive amplitude from projector walkers. SR makes the likelihood update
transfer between independent populations, but the resulting fixed-node energy
is not yet reproducible over repeated iterations. The method also requires a
useful starting amplitude and should not be confused with repeated bare
Krylov iteration, which is worse on $6\times6$ (Appendix~\ref{app:k2}).

\section{Direct second Krylov step}
\label{app:k2}

On $6\times6$, retaining the full first Krylov amplitude gives
$a_1(x)=|T_1-r_0(x)|a_0(x)$ with coefficient of variation about $0.18$.
A second label-free threshold changes only $0.8$--$2.0\%$ of the
$|\psi_{\rm ViT}|^2$ probability but raises the fixed-amplitude energy by
$0.65$--$0.96$. The feedback construction therefore re-solves the amplitude
before another sign update.

\section{Statistical conventions}
\label{app:stats}

Exact-diagonalization data are deterministic up to eigensolver precision.
For $6\times6$ fixed-amplitude energies we quote between-chain standard
errors from 64 chains. For fixed-node data with two independent walker
populations, error bars denote half the population difference and are
reproducibility scales only; the $6\times6$ Marshall value uses the standard
error over eight populations.
