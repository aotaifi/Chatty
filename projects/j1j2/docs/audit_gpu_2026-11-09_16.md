# GPU audit, j1j2, 2026-11-09 .. 2026-11-16 (independent, read-only)

Question (PI): is GPU time spent on thought-through tests, or on blind / brute-force testing?

Short answer: mostly thought-through. About two thirds of the theorie GPU-h and about 57% of the Paderborn H100-h went to
jobs that had a written prediction and kill rule before submission and whose result changed a decision. The avoidable
spend is not blind testing. It comes from three process gaps: racing or backup copies left running after their twin had
finished, grading runs whose resolution could not reach their own threshold (computable on paper beforehand), and a few
re-learned lessons. The "train longer, try a variant" pattern described in CLAUDE.md:14-20 shows up only in small amounts
(0.4 A40-h).

## 4. Process changes (at most 5)

1. **A twin is cancelled when its sibling finishes.** Every racing or backup copy is submitted together with a watcher that
   cancels the slower twin, keyed on the first done=true. It runs as the owner's own pre-approved cancel, with no per-cancel PI word
   (the rule in dt:1442/1450 is what left 4182798 and 4186183 to run). A "run the gate by hand" verdict on a twin wakes the owner.
   Prevents: #1 ctrl_c0 (24.0 A40-h), #4 prodB (~3.4 H100-h), #7 s307 backups (~1.9 H100-h), and most of r1p_slice.
2. **A runtime check in the launcher.** `steps x measured s/step (same card, same kernel) <= 0.5 x fit_sec` is asserted before
   sbatch, using the last logged rate for that card. If no rate exists, the job is a probe. Prevents: #1 (70.9 s/step was on
   record), #8 sbad0a timeout.
3. **A power line in every ANALYSIS_BRIEF before submit.** The brief states sigma(gated quantity) at the planned blocks, chains and
   samples, the largest physically possible effect, and the quotable.py minimum (256 blocks/chain). If 2 sigma > the effect,
   or below the minimum, the run is redesigned on paper (paired, more chains) or not run. The lead's new LS:49 states this.
   Here it should be a launcher refusal, not a lesson. Prevents: #2 headline B (12.1 H100-h), #3 T-CAP8 (9.3 A40-h), #5,
   #6 R0, #9, #12.
4. **Open inconsistencies block grades of the same kind.** A recorded "error bars suspect" or "cause not understood" on an
   estimator (PP:2279) becomes a gate. No further grade with that estimator and chain count runs until the cause is
   understood or the chain count is raised to cover it. Prevents: #2 headline B. It would also have moved the 8-chain rule
   forward by 3 days.
5. **Before a fit on a new pool, or a retrained stored sign, run a CPU check of the recorded failure modes.** Grep LESSONS and
   TRIED_IDEAS for the mechanism and run the CPU counterfactual of the named terms (near-node d ln B ~ 1/B; trained signs worse than the
   untrained sign, LS:13/16). Prevents: part of R1' (slice 10.9 A40-h, ~10 H100-h) and T-SOFT2 (5.0 A40-h). Both are "unclear",
   so the saving is uncertain.

## What works, and should not change

- Pre-registration is real. Almost every test above 1 GPU-h has a prediction and kill rule committed before its submit time
  (K=3, G(-5), T-BAD, CONTROL C, rho probes, T-STRONG s0, S1/S2, T-VARDEC, T-BAD10, R1' seal 608e31d). Several were sealed with
  numbers that the results then hit (rho 0.977 sealed vs 0.978 measured, dt:1834) or missed with a diagnosis (R1').
- Sizing probes before production (placement table, timing runs, memory checks; ~1.7 A40-h) prevented larger mistakes, e.g. the B arm
  stayed on Paderborn after the 294 s/block slice probe (dt:1795-1797).
- Kills are honoured and stop the line of work. No variant runs after T-NODE, T-SOFT2 (apart from the 0.4 h retune), G(-5) or T-STRONG s0;
  the cheap CPU follow-ups (S3, T-VARDEC, GN share) come before any new GPU.
- Duplicates are caught and corrected in the record (dt:1629-1630), and lessons are written the same day.
- Cheap CPU work answers questions before GPU in many places (T-CERT, T-TRACE, T-ROUND3, H-loc, relabel pilot, identity
  tests).
