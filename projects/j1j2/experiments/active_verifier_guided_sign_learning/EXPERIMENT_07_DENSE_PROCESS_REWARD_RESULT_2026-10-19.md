# AVSL-07 — Dense process-reward exploration test (2026-10-19)

**Result: NEGATIVE FOR THIS PROCESS PROXY.** A cheap randomized 4-bond estimate of the local signed off-diagonal energy, applied as intermediate reward during a two-hop exploratory policy, did not improve held-out rare positive-orbit discovery relative to matched terminal-only group-RL, a shuffled-process counterfactual, or random exploration. This does **not** rule out verifier-guided process supervision in frustrated QMC.

## Hypothesis

20-site post-Krylov exact FN signs have rare individual-orbit beneficial corrections. Standard GRPO/DAPO-inspired terminal binary reward leaves approximately 90% of four-proposal groups with no positive example. Can **dense process feedback from a few observed Hamiltonian bonds after the FIRST of TWO policy moves** improve a learned frontier-generation policy without requiring a full Krylov or full symmetry-orbit gain query at that intermediate state?

### Precisely defined proxy and training

Given first-hop configuration `x1`, sample `m=4` uniform legal Hamiltonian neighbors `y` and construct

```
R_proc(x1) = (1/m) Σ_sampled_edges [ 2 H_{x1,y} s(x1)s(y) a(y)/a(x1) ].
```

It unbiasedly estimates the **per-edge mean** offdiagonal signed local-energy contribution at `x1`, not `ΔE` for flipping its symmetry orbit. It is *not independent ground truth*. It does **not** include physical weight `a(x1)^2` or remove internal symmetry-orbit bonds. This mismatch is an important reason it might fail. A 4-edge query needs positive guide amplitudes and signs for the selected endpoints; every additional query was counted. Full-orbit terminal gain is only computed after the second action, and always scored for **all attempted actions**, including unsuccessful/filtered groups.

All policies use an epsilon-mixed 9-feature **linear stochastic geometry policy**, `epsilon=0.15`, group size 4, two hops, normalized within-group rewards and clipped gradient step. **These are conceptual transfers from group-RL/process supervision, NOT full DAPO clipping/token loss or an LLM-scale implementation.** No ED ground-state sign labels are used.

For dense-reward arms the first move gets a normalized within-group process advantage, while both moves receive a normalized terminal success advantage when groups have at least one positive and one negative; the signed process-weight is 0.5. Terminal success is `G_orbit>1e-12` at **fixed exact 20-site FN amplitude**, and a failed flip is never physically accepted merely for having positive relative advantage.

Controls:
- **binary**: terminal-only group-standardized policy (mixed reward groups only);
- **dense_signed**: the actual 4-edge signed-bond process reward;
- **dense_shuffled**: same guide calls, same sampled signed rewards, but randomly permuted among four actions to destroy causal alignment;
- **dense_geometry**: cheap intermediate site-geometry signal, no amplitude/sign process oracle;
- **keep_relative**: terminal binary keep-relative policy;
- **random / geometry / novelty / signed_bond**: no learned policy baselines. The signed-bond action-selection heuristic explicitly pays its cost of examining all candidates on each move.

## Protocol
- Model: tilted 20-site spin-1/2 J1–J2, J2/J1=0.5, sector dimension 184756; frozen **post-Krylov FN amplitude/sign guide** regenerated by the archived reference source.
- Six independent train/test root seed blocks 10019–10024, with four root-chain seeds per block and disjoint root generators for train/eval. `1,200` **attempted** training trajectories per method per block; `160` independent held-out attempted trajectories per method/block (960 in aggregate per method). Individual symmetry orbits can repeat between training/evaluation: **NOT a held-out-orbit transfer test.**
- 4 candidate trajectories per learning group; 300 training groups per policy per block.
- Toy amplitude-tempered `pi∝a` root chains (NOT actual FN mixed walkers), exact guide amplitude + signs used on-demand, no scalable NQS backend and no post-FN reprojected energy. Comparator roots common, per-arm caches/counters independent.
- Each training arm incurs full 1200 terminal attempts. Extra guide calls for dense_signed/dense_shuffled are included, not free.
- Numeric target is **number of distinct positive single-orbit proposals discovered**, not sum of positive independent-orbit gains (which is not joint physical improvement). Independent full-H sign flip identity was validated in the earlier 20-site experiment, and 17 tests covering process reward API, gauge/global-sign invariance, sampled edges, rollout, reward grouping, direct-H gain scorer and independent verifier pass.

## Results (6 seed blocks, 960 held-out attempts/method)

| Method | Positive distinct orbits | Mean best single-orbit gain, total energy | Mean train unique amplitude configurations per seed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| binary group RL | 25 | 2.44161e-5 | 21,060.5 |
| **dense signed-bond feedback** | **21** | 8.07904e-6 | **25,642.3** |
| shuffled signed feedback | 25 | 4.52082e-5 | 25,558.8 |
| dense geometry | 23 | 2.34256e-5 | 21,275.3 |
| keep-relative binary RL | 30 | 4.70460e-5 | 21,001.3 |
| **random exploration, without training** | **31** | 3.18533e-5 | no training |
| geometry-only heuristic | 19 | 1.39288e-5 | no training |
| novelty heuristic | 18 | 2.05351e-5 | no training |
| signed-bond candidate-scoring heuristic | 5 | 4.38693e-5 | no training; evaluation needs ~16,220 unique amplitudes/seed instead of ~5,760 |

All arms' **held-out** evaluation typically consume ~5,650–5,770 unique 20-site amplitude oracle configurations per seed; the signed-bond heuristic is much more expensive even though it trains no network.

- Dense signed feedback demanded **+21.76%** more distinct training guide-amplitude queries than the binary group RL.
- Across six matched test-root blocks: `dense_signed - random` positives per block = `[-3, 0, -2, -1, -2, -2]`, mean **-1.67** per 160 evaluations. Descriptive Student-t 95% interval for block differences `[-2.75,-0.58]`; tiny six-block sample, shared physics chamber, and multiple arms mean the interval is **not** a formal discovery p-value.
- `dense_signed - binary` per block = `[-6,+2,-4,+6,0,-2]`, mean `-0.67`, descriptive 95% interval `[-5.20,+3.87]`. No reliable positive advantage.
- Training binary success is rare in every arm: in dense-signed training 184 / 7200 proposals positive; random train terminal opportunities often vanish in entire groups. The proxy creates nonzero feedback in many all-negative groups, but not useful feedback about final success.

## Independent held-out process calibration

Separate toy sampler seeds `81001–81004`, **512 random two-hop rollouts**. Terminal labels and first-intermediate-orbit gain were queried *only after* generating the 4-edge process proxy, and never used to update policy parameters.

- Positive **intermediate-orbit** labels: 11/512. Four-bond proxy AUC for their detection: **0.469** (near chance).
- Positive **terminal-orbit** labels: 10/512. AUC for these labels: **0.623**, highly uncertain at n=10; proxy-vs-terminal continuous Spearman **0.060**.
- The 4-edge sampled signed quantity is internally consistent as a randomized estimator of the all-legal-edge mean: over 800 repetitions on three fixed states, observed deviations were `|z| = 1.35,1.27,0.07` sampling SEM. This tests the *proxy's own correctness*, not predictive validity.
- Interpretation: the intermediate reward seems weakly related to physical *sign-flip success*. In particular, density is useless if its gradients optimize the wrong thing.

## Decision and next physically informed option

**Reject this particular unweighted local signed-bond pressure as an intermediate RL reward** and do **not** simply tune its weight, increase model size, or run additional 6x6/8x8 training. Dense process rewards are not intrinsically helpful; they must be **causally aligned with the actual sign-correction goal**.

One sharper, falsifiable next test is to construct a *bounded unbiased partial estimator of the actual symmetry-orbit boundary gain* rather than local signed pressure: include amplitude weight `a(x)^2`, correct symmetry multiplicity, and exclude orbit-internal bonds, using a sampled subset of boundary edges. **First independently calibrate its held-out precision/positive predictive value and incremental query cost**; only train a new policy if the signal predicts rare true-positive corrections substantially better than chance and remains cheap. Such a teacher still needs a scalable orbit-local implementation.

Alternative from LLM research: train a **process reward model (PRM)** to predict future terminal success from observations at each step, holding out entire root trajectories and sign chambers; do not mistake self-generated reward for an independent verifier. The ultimate criterion is improvement in held-out candidate discovery **per total including source training cost**, then a callable local sign update with independent fixed-a and post-FN energy audits.
