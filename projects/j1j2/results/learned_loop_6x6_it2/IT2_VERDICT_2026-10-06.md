## Why it fails: the amplitude limits lattice FN, not the signs
Oracle FN guides were run with the same protocol. The FN code returns E0 exactly, with zero variance, for (|psi0|, sgn psi0).

| guide (amplitude, sign) | E_FN | pops |
|---|---|---|
| ViT guide | -0.503670(8) | 400 |
| (\|ViT\|, exact signs) | -0.503682(21) | 128 |
| (\|psi0\|, ViT signs) | -0.503731(15) | 32 |
| (a1, exact signs) | -0.503652(29) | 96 |
| (a2, exact signs) | -0.503649(23) | 96 |

- Even exact signs do not lower E_FN at the ViT amplitude. The reason is how lattice FN treats sign-violating (frustrated) edges: it moves them to the diagonal, weighted by a(y)/a(x). So the remaining error near the ViT is set by the amplitude.
- The exact amplitude with the ViT signs is 6e-5 lower than the ViT guide.
- The learned amplitudes a1 and a2 are not better than |ViT| under oracle signs. std(log a - log|psi0|) is 0.061-0.062 for a1 and a2, against 0.048 for |ViT|.

## Sign machinery (works, and is cheap)
- A stacked stored net is a lossy store of a hop:
  - D2 has w_s 4.9e-4. The ss6 K2 stacked net has w_s 5.75e-4.
  - These nets miss about 43% of the rare flips.
- Stored net + one exact hop is as good as the recursion:
  - G1 in the form K1 net + hop has w_s 6.5e-5. The recursive s^(2) has 8e-5.
  - On the 5e4-sample subset they give the same 1.0e-4.
- The third step on a lossy stored base reaches w_s 4.25e-5 (ViT 1.3e-4). The hop repairs the base.
- Every sign evaluation costs one hop, regardless of iteration. FN on a hop guide costs 40-75 s per population.
- Threshold T: on 32768 samples, the raw estimate, the smoothed estimate and the half-sample estimates still scatter between -1 and -6.5. But the held-out dH changes by less than 1e-5/site over that range. The energy curve is flat, so T is ill-determined but irrelevant to the energy.

## Amplitude step (frozen H_FN SR, fresh samples, validation gate that does not stop at the first rejection)
- a2main (8192 + 8192 samples per step, A40): the first 3 steps were rejected, giving no update.
- a2small (3072 + 4096 per step, 2080Ti): 6 of 9 steps accepted.
  - Independent check on 16384 + 16384 samples: dL = -7.4(5.3)e-5 and -0.2(1.8)e-5. Neither is resolved. RMS of delta log a is 0.006.
- The maximum available gain was <H>_G1 - E_FN[G1] = 2.5(5.3)e-5.
- Exact-loop contraction is about 0.85-0.89 per iteration (6x6 exact loop; 4x4 net-start). That is about 1.5e-5/site per ideal iteration at the ViT level.
- Beating the ViT by 2 sigma would need about 5 ideal iterations. Each learned iteration resolves only about 1e-4.
- Anderson mixing on log a was not tried: it needs at least 3 resolvable outer iterates.

## FN referee caveat
- M=128 populations have a heavy low tail. Below E0: 65/400 for the ViT guide, 16-17/64 for the hop guides.
- One M=512 population fell 1e-3 below E0.
- Claims at the 3e-5 level need M >= 512, medians or trimmed means, and the ViT guide on the same seeds. Common-random-number pairing gives a correlation of only about 0.1.

## Next step (justified)
On 6x6 the loop's remaining lever is the amplitude on frustrated edges. Signs are already better than the ViT's (4e-5 vs 1.3e-4) and do not help further. Two options:
1. Make the amplitude update converge like the exact map, for example by fitting phi_FN directly from long FN walker histories with many samples. This must gain more than about 3e-5 per iteration under a robust (M=512) referee.
2. Test whether the oracle conclusion holds at 8x8. If it does, the paper's claim should be "a Krylov sign correction that matches the best network at one-hop cost", not "beats the best network".
