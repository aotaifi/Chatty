Problem. Spin-1/2 J1-J2 Heisenberg model, square lattice 6x6 periodic, J2/J1 = 0.5. We alternate (i) a Krylov sign step and (ii) lattice fixed-node (FN) projection with a guide psi_G = s(x) a(x). FN gives an improved positive amplitude phi_FN (exact on 6x6 via ED in the symmetric sector). We need to write phi_FN back into a callable network a_theta (start: a symmetrised ViT, 155k params, 1.3e-4 per site above E0). The energy cost of a write-back error delta = log a_theta - log phi_FN is the exact edge-difference form Q = 1/2 sum_{x,y} |F_xy| phi_x phi_y (delta_x - delta_y)^2 (F = frozen FN Hamiltonian).

Findings: the needed correction per FN iteration is small (rms 0.011 in log a) but rough (white-noise-like in the Q metric) and tail-concentrated: 81% of the gain sits on 7.6% of the phi^2 weight; half the gain requires accurate corrections on configurations with phi^2 < 1e-11 (>1.2M symmetry orbits). With EXACT phi_FN as data: a residual CNN on top of the frozen ViT, trained on the Q loss with Adam, captures 15-18% of one iteration with phi^2 sampling, 31% with a tail-weighted proposal and a 111k-param net (capture grows with capacity); minSR on the residual 5-18%; Gauss-Newton in the Q metric overfits each batch; minSR fine-tuning the ViT itself raised the frozen-FN energy. Simple guide-derived features capture 3-25%. Plain VMC (SR) on the same nets gains <1% at realistic sample sizes. A one-step Lanczos improvement of the ViT gains far more (1.32e-4 -> 2.55e-5) but is a non-network state.
